Proof of delivery (POD) is a document or set of data that proves the goods were delivered as agreed: a signature, a photo, the time and location, or a barcode scan. Without it, unpaid freight comes down to your word against the customer's, and a slip of paper is easily lost. This article shows which form of POD suits which type of freight, from pallets to containers, and how to get paid faster.

What is proof of delivery, and why does it decide disputes over money?

Proof of delivery (POD) is evidence of delivery: it shows that the goods arrived, when, to whom and in what condition. In practice, it is a set of items collected by the driver, such as the consignee's signature, photos, the time and location of delivery or a barcode scan, which supports the invoice. In international road haulage, the consignee's signature on the CMR consignment note is the traditional POD.

The three jobs of a POD: delivery, receivable, evidence

A POD does three things at once. It closes the delivery, triggers invoicing and serves as evidence if the customer denies receiving the goods. Without a POD, the invoice rests on memory and trust.

Why a slip of paper is no longer enough

A slip of paper gets lost, gets wet or stays in the glovebox until the dispute is already a month old. A digital POD is saved at the moment of delivery with the time and location, so the evidence exists before anyone has a chance to question anything. Which forms of POD suit which type of freight?

The rule is simple: the POD must prove exactly what the dispute is about. With palletised freight, disputes are about quantity and condition; with swap units, about time and place; with parcels, about whether an individual item arrived. A summary below.

Palletised freightSignature and notes of discrepanciesThe consignee counts and checks the pallets on site
Trailer swapTime, location and photos of the unit and sealThere is often nobody at the swap point to sign

Palletised freight: signature and notes of discrepancies

A signature is still the strongest evidence when the consignee is present. Discrepancies, such as broken packaging, missing packages and water damage, are noted at the time of signing. If the consignee accepts the goods without reservations, in CMR carriage the goods are deemed, unless proven otherwise, to have been delivered in the condition described in the consignment note. Reservations about apparent damage must be made no later than at delivery, and about other damage in writing within seven days (Finnish Road Transport Contracts Act 345/1979, section 40, Finlex).

Trailer swaps and containers: time, location and photos

There is usually nobody at a swap yard or terminal gate to sign. The time and location recorded by the app show that the unit was at the agreed place at the agreed time, and photos of its sides and seal show its condition. That is enough for many disputes, because they are about time and place, not about the contents of the load.

Express and parcel freight: barcode and photo

A barcode scan shows that this particular item was delivered to this consignee. A photo serves as back-up for deliveries where the consignee is not present: the door, the address and the item in front of it. The combination stands up in a dispute where the consignee claims nothing arrived.

How does a digital POD protect you in a freight dispute?

What makes a POD usable as evidence

When a customer disputes a delivery, a slip of paper in a desk drawer is weak evidence: it may be hard to find, it does not always show the time or place, and the signature may be illegible. A digital POD is strong evidence when it contains three things: a time and place recorded by the system itself, identifiable details of the signatory, such as name and signature, and storage that cannot be altered afterwards. The same idea underlies the CMR consignment note: under Article 9 of the Convention, the consignment note is prima facie evidence of the contract of carriage, its conditions and the receipt of the goods.

Typical disputes and missing evidence

Three disputes keep recurring in a small firm. The customer says the delivery never came, even though the driver unloaded the goods. The customer claims damage that nobody photographed during unloading. Or the consignee recorded a different quantity from the consignment note, and the only evidence is the driver's recollection from a week ago.

A digital POD helps in all of them in the same way: a photo of the damage with time and place, the consignee's signature on the phone screen, and everything saved against this particular transport. When you receive a complaint, you reply with a single message containing the photos, signature, time and place. Many disputes end there, because the other side can see you are not guessing. If the matter goes to debt collection or to court, evidence that is timestamped and tied to a location is much stronger than a slip of paper.

Checklist: what to do when a customer does not pay?

Before delivery

Agree the freight rate, payment terms and late payment interest in writing. Between businesses, under the Finnish Interest Act, late payment interest is the reference rate plus eight percentage points, unless otherwise agreed. Make sure the loading list and delivery address match the order. If the load is out of the ordinary, for example wet, fragile or requiring special unloading, record it before departure.

At the time of delivery

The driver collects a POD suited to the freight: signature and photos for palletised freight; time, location and photos of the trailer or container. Discrepancies are recorded immediately on the consignment note and in photos, not afterwards. If the consignee refuses to sign, the driver records the refusal, the time and the place, and reports it to the office straight away. Never leave the site without any evidence.

When a dispute flares up

Send the invoice and the POD together as soon as possible, preferably within 24 hours, because many disputes arise from paperwork arriving weeks late. Call before sending a payment reminder: many disputes are resolved when the customer's accounts payable clerk sees the signed POD. If payment still does not come, send a written payment reminder and then pass the debt to collection. Document every step: dates, calls and attachments. Without a POD you have an opinion; with a POD you have a document.

How does the POD connect to invoicing and tracking receivables?

The POD as an attachment to the invoice

When the POD is digital, it can be sent as an attachment to the invoice: consignment note, signature and photos in one package. The customer cannot claim they did not receive the delivery document, because it came with the invoice. This removes the most common cause of delay, where an invoice waits weeks for a "missing paper".

Tracking unpaid invoices without a paper memo

Once the delivery has been signed for, you know the transport is ready to invoice, and you can track the invoice up to the due date. You can see at a glance who owes you money and for how long, without a separate memo or spreadsheet.

What does a POD require of a driver who speaks another language, and of the phone?

A simple interface

For the driver, a POD must take just a few taps: take photos, ask for a signature, mark as done. In a team that speaks several languages, long text forms cause mistakes, so clear buttons and the driver's own language work better. The signature is drawn with a finger on the phone screen, and no paper needs to be scanned at the office afterwards. The driver should see only the details of their own task, in their own language, not the whole system.

Without a connection at ports and warehouses

At ports, warehouse yards and in remote areas, the signal often drops. A POD app must store the photo, time and location on the phone and send them automatically when the connection returns. If a confirmation is lost because of a network outage, that is exactly the load most likely to be disputed later. FAQ

Can a POD be sent by text message or email, or is an app needed?

You can send a POD by email or message, but then the photo stays in a message thread without a system-recorded time or a link to this particular transport. An app records the time, place and transport automatically. Email works as a fallback, not as the main method, because a broken chain of evidence weakens your position in a dispute.

How long must POD evidence be kept?

Under section 41 of the Finnish Road Transport Contracts Act and the CMR Convention, an action concerning carriage must be brought within one year, or within three years in cases of wilful misconduct or gross negligence. When the POD is attached to an invoice, it forms part of the accounting records, and under the Finnish Accounting Act vouchers must be kept for at least six years from the end of the year in which the financial year ended (Accounting Act 1336/1997, Finlex). The safest course is therefore to keep proofs of delivery for at least six years.

Does POD confirmation work without a connection, for example at a port or warehouse?

Yes, provided the app stores the photo, time and location on the phone and sends them automatically when the connection returns. It is precisely in places without a signal that confirmations are easily lost, and precisely those loads that are disputed later. Test how the app works offline before you rely on it.

What do I do if the customer disputes the consignee's signature?

Reply with a single message containing the full evidence package: the signature and the consignee's name, the time, the location and the photos. Because the system recorded the time and place itself at the moment of delivery, it is hard for the customer to justify the dispute. If the matter goes to debt collection, evidence that is timestamped and tied to a location is strong proof.

Is a digital POD also suitable for transports given to a subcontractor?

Yes, and it is especially useful then: when the subcontractor's driver confirms delivery in the same way, you have evidence even though the transport was not run with your own vehicle. You can show the customer that the delivery took place, and separate your own liability from the subcontractor's performance. Make a POD requirement a condition of the subcontracting agreement.
