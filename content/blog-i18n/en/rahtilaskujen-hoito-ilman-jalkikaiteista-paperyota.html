This guide goes through the whole life cycle from order to archiving, including loose trailer and freight exchange jobs.

What does freight invoicing without paperwork after the fact mean?

A paperless freight invoicing process means that the job details are created once in the system and go straight from there into the invoice: the order, route, driver's sign-off, POD attachment and consignment note details in a single chain. The invoice is produced ready for the archive, and you do not have to spend an evening going through envelopes of receipts and notes.

For a small business the benefit is tangible: when dispatching, invoicing and bookkeeping are all handled by the same person, every line typed by hand takes time that is lost elsewhere. According to the Finnish Tax Administration, an electronic invoice must contain the same mandatory details as a paper invoice, so the law does not force you to use paper (Finnish Tax Administration). In RAHTIS the shipper publishes the job once and the driver signs off each stage directly, so the invoice material is ready before the lorry is back at base. In practice the process starts with the customer's job or the freight exchange job being entered once, not in three places.

The essence of going paperless is not the technology itself but joining the information flows into a single chain in which the same data is never entered twice. When the driver signs off the delivery in the mobile app, the sign-off does not disappear into an inbox waiting for a separate invoicing round; it triggers the invoice automatically. This means the invoice can be in the customer's inbox or online bank before the driver has even made it back home. With the traditional model this would be impossible, because invoicing always depends on the paper consignment note coming back and the data being entered into the system by hand. In a paperless process that delay disappears and the invoicing lead time shrinks from days to hours, which significantly improves the company's cash flow.

What requirements apply to an electronic freight invoice in Finland?

Mandatory invoice details

The Finnish VAT Act sets out what every freight invoice must contain: the names and details of the seller and the buyer, the Business ID or other identifier, the invoice number, the date, the date or period of supply, the quantity and nature of the goods or services, the VAT rate, the amount payable and the payment terms. Whether the job comes via a freight exchange or from your own customer, the same fields are filled in every time. In RAHTIS the order and consignment note details serve as the source data, so the mandatory fields fill themselves in without a separate paper form. If you invoice abroad, for example a Swedish or German customer, the details are the same, but the language and references are worth checking at the order stage. In addition to the mandatory invoice details, the haulage sector has its own specific requirements that general invoicing software does not always cover. A freight invoice often includes a reference number linking it to a particular job or transport contract, plus a breakdown of surcharges such as waiting time, unloading delays or ADR surcharges. Itemising these by hand on a paper invoice or in general invoicing software is laborious and error-prone. When the source data comes straight from the transport management system, the breakdown is generated automatically from the prices agreed with the order and the extra events recorded during the transport. This reduces customers' questions about the content of the invoice and speeds up payment.

Archiving and retention period

Under the Finnish Accounting Act, invoices and accounting material must be kept for at least six years from the end of the financial year, and the financial statements material for seven years. Electronic archiving is permitted as long as the data remains unaltered and accessible throughout the retention period. In practice this means POD attachments, consignment note details and invoices can live in the same system without cardboard files. When the driver's sign-off and attachment have been linked to the job during the transport, archiving happens at the same time, and no one is left with paperwork after the fact.

For retention requirements, what matters is that the electronic archive is protected against changes and its data is traceable. The system must be able to show when the invoice was created, when the attachments were added and who signed them off. RAHTIS stores the time stamp and user for every event, so the audit trail is in order without a separate log book. If the tax authority asks for evidence of a particular job from three years ago, the whole set is in one place: the invoice, consignment note, POD attachment and the driver's sign-off with time stamps. In the paper world the same job might be scattered across three or four files in different offices, and some of the information could be missing or hard to read.

How do you get freight invoice data straight from the transport management system into the invoice?

Order stage: getting the job details right once

The chain starts with the order: the customer's email or a freight exchange job is turned into a transport task in the system, with the collection and delivery addresses, load description, freight rate, surcharges (such as waiting time or ADR) and payment terms. This data is saved once and reused at every stage: for the consignment note, the driver's task list and finally the invoice. Eliminating retyping is the core of the whole chain, because every line copied by hand is one more possible error on the invoice and one more reason for the customer to pay late.

In practice this means that when you enter a job in RAHTIS, the same record goes with it all the way: from the order to the consignment note details, the driver's sign-offs and the finished invoice line. You do not have to dig the agreed price out of a notebook later or check scraps of paper, because everything is already in the same record. The system does not replace dispatching decisions; it simply makes sure that data entered once is not lost on its way to the invoice.

The quality of the data created at the order stage determines whether the whole chain works. If an address is incomplete or the freight rate is missing, these gaps carry through to the consignment note and the invoice, so the first entry has to be made carefully. In practice it pays to create order templates for different types of customer: a regular customer with fixed routes and prices, a freight exchange job where prices vary, and a one-off job where everything is entered by hand. Once the template is ready, the mandatory fields are filled in by default and only exceptions need manual input. This halves the time spent on orders and reduces the risk of entry errors, because the system will not let a job go forward without the mandatory details.

Consignment note details as the basis for the invoice

The consignment note (CMR) is filled in from the same record, and once the driver signs off the delivery and the POD attachment has been saved, the invoice is produced without a separate step. The invoice shows the customer details, freight rate, surcharges and attachments, all derived from the original order and the events of the transport. Retention obligations are best checked directly in official guidance, such as on the Finnish Tax Administration's website, so that archiving is done correctly from the start.

The consignment note details form the basis of the invoice because they contain what actually happened during the transport: loading and unloading times, any damage or discrepancies, and the consignee's acknowledgement. When this data comes straight from the system, the invoice is based not just on the order but on verifiable events. If the customer claims the delivery was late, the time stamp of unloading attached to the invoice shows when it actually happened. If the freight rate includes waiting time, the driver's sign-off verifies how long the wait lasted. This chain from order to consignment note to invoice is what makes freight invoicing without paperwork after the fact possible: every invoice line can be traced to a concrete event, not to a note or a receipt.

How are the driver's sign-offs and attachments added to a digital freight invoice?

POD and CMR sign-off on mobile

Straight after unloading, the driver photographs the signed CMR or consignment note on their phone and marks the load as delivered in the app. The photo, time stamp and location are saved directly to that job, so paper copies do not have to be posted back or scanned at the office in the evening. If the consignee insists on a paper acknowledgement, a photographed document is enough as an invoice attachment for most customers.

How easy the driver's mobile app is to use determines how likely the sign-off is to be done correctly and on time. If the app is slow or needs lots of fields filled in, the driver puts the sign-off off until the evening or forgets it altogether, and invoicing is delayed. The RAHTIS mobile sign-off is designed around the bare minimum: the job opens, the driver takes a photo, the consignee signs on the screen and the job is marked as complete. The location and time stamp are saved in the background with no extra step. This means the sign-off is done before the driver starts the engine at the delivery point, and the invoice material is ready straight away.

Automatically linking attachments to the invoice

When the job is marked as complete, the system gathers the POD photo, the CMR sign-off and any other attachments into the same record from which the invoice is produced. The invoicing software does not have to ask for attachments separately afterwards, and they do not need to be attached by hand via email. In RAHTIS the driver's sign-off is visible to the customer step by step, and the completed POD is attached to the invoice automatically (https://www.rahtis.eu/lt). This removes the most common reason for payment disputes: the customer cannot claim that the delivery document is missing.

An honest limitation: if the customer requires the original paper CMR to be posted, a digital image only partly replaces it, and the original must still be kept.

Automatically linking attachments means in practice that the invoice is not a separate document but part of the job record. When the invoice goes to the customer, the POD attachment, the CMR photo and any additional explanations go with it, without anyone having to put them together separately. This is a significant difference from the traditional model, where invoicing is a separate step that needs its own attachments, its own email and its own archiving. In RAHTIS these are parts of the same record, and sending the invoice takes one click because all the supporting material is already in place.

How do loose trailer and freight exchange jobs fit into the same invoicing process?

Loose trailer work should not be a separate invoicing channel. When freight exchange jobs and your own customers go through the same system, filling empty legs and invoicing are handled without a notebook or a paper CMR.

Freight exchange job details straight into the system

When you take a job from a freight exchange, the customer details, route and freight are entered once, not afterwards from a receipt. In RAHTIS the driver signs off collection and delivery, the POD attachment is saved with the job and the consignment note details complete the invoice template. Electronic consignment notes are permitted in the EU under the Additional Protocol to the CMR Convention (UNECE e-CMR page), so there is no need to carry paper.

The catch with loose trailer jobs is that customers change and invoice terms vary: one pays at 14 days, another at 30 days, a third requires its own reference number system. When this information is recorded with the job, the invoice adapts automatically to each customer's requirements without you having to remember who pays on which terms. This is particularly important when you run a mix of jobs: your own customers, jobs from Trans.eu and jobs agreed directly by phone, all in the same week. If invoicing is a separate process, you have to check each job's terms individually, which takes longer than the driving itself.

Invoicing several customers

Each customer receives its own invoice with its own terms and references, even if the jobs are in the same calendar. When the POD and consignment note are already attached, the invoice can be sent as soon as the goods are delivered, and a later empty leg that gets filled is handled in the same way. Managing several customers requires the system to keep jobs clearly apart: who ordered it, what the route is, what the price is and when the invoice falls due. When this information is in the same calendar, you can see at a glance which invoices have been sent and which are waiting. This prevents double invoicing and forgotten jobs, which are the most common mistakes in a paper-based process. And when filling empty legs is part of the same system, invoicing those legs follows the same logic: the driver signs off the collection, the invoice is produced and the POD is attached automatically.

How are archiving and inspections handled without a paper archive?

When freight invoice data, the driver's sign-offs and POD attachments build up in the same system, the archive creates itself as you work. Every job remains searchable: the consignment note details, attachments and invoice can be found by customer or registration number in seconds, with no safe or old files.

Payment tracking without a notebook

You can see the status of each invoice directly in the system: sent, paid, overdue. Instead of recording "who owes what" in a paper notebook, RAHTIS tracks payment dates job by job and flags the overdue ones. You can send payment reminders without a separate Excel file, and your accountant gets the data straight from the system instead of scraps of paper.

Effective payment tracking depends on the system knowing the status of every invoice in real time. When the invoice goes out, the status is "sent". When payment arrives, it changes to "paid". If the due date passes, the status changes to "overdue" and the system flags it without you having to go through a list by hand. This is a significant advantage over Excel-based tracking, where the status has to be updated by hand and overdue invoices can go unnoticed for weeks. In RAHTIS overdue invoices appear in a separate view from which you can send a payment reminder with one click, without having to look up the original invoice or the customer's contact details separately.

Responding to inspections from a digital archive

When the tax authority or customs asks for documents, digging through years-old piles of paper becomes a search. You select the period and the customer, and the system lists the consignment notes, POD attachments and invoices. The same applies to disputes with customers: if the other party claims a delivery is missing, the sign-off time stamp and the POD attachment can be shown in moments. RAHTIS keeps the archive in order automatically, but final responsibility for retention obligations and tax treatment lies with you and your accountant, as with any system.

In an inspection, speed and completeness are what count. If the tax authority gives you a week to supply invoices and related documents for a three-month period, putting them together from a paper archive can take a whole day. From a digital archive the same material is put together in minutes: you select the period, filter by customer and the system lists everything, including attachments. This not only saves time but also reduces the risk of errors, because documents can get lost or mixed up when gathered by hand. What is more, the digital archive is always in the same format and in the same place, so responding to different inspectors' requests is consistent and quick.

What should you bear in mind when moving from paper to electronic freight invoices?

Making the switch in 30 days

Week 1: choose one customer and one type of job for a pilot. Week 2: enter open jobs into the system and link the consignment note details directly to the invoice. Week 3: introduce driver sign-offs and POD attachments so the attachments follow the invoice automatically. Week 4: invoice the pilot customer electronically and check what is missing before moving everything else across.

Keep a paper CMR in the vehicle as a backup for the first month. Finnish Customs and the Finnish Tax Administration may require transport documents during inspections, so check the retention periods directly on the Finnish Tax Administration's website.

A successful switch depends on the driver learning the new way quickly and the customer accepting a digital invoice without resistance. For the driver, go through the mobile sign-off steps before the first job: open the app, select the job, take a photo, ask for a signature, mark as complete. Once learnt, the whole thing takes 30 seconds. For the customer, agree in advance that the invoice will come electronically with a photographed CMR attached rather than a paper one. Most customers accept this because it speeds up their own invoice processing. If a customer insists on a paper invoice, it is worth checking whether this is a real requirement or just habit, since in most cases it is habit rather than a legal requirement.

FAQ

How much time should paperless invoicing save a small firm?

This guide does not give an exact number of minutes, but the saving comes from data entered once not being copied again from the order to the consignment note and the invoice. When dispatching, invoicing and collecting POD attachments are handled in the same chain, going through and scanning envelopes of receipts in the afternoons disappears altogether, and the invoice can be sent as soon as the goods are delivered.

Is a photographed CMR enough as an invoice attachment?

For most customers it is, but not for all. If the consignee or customer requires the original paper CMR, a digital image only partly replaces it, and the original document must still be kept. That is why it is worth keeping a paper CMR in the vehicle as a backup, especially during the first month of the switch, and agreeing the method of sign-off with the customer in advance.

How long must invoices and attachments be kept?

Under the Finnish Accounting Act, invoices and accounting material are kept for at least six years from the end of the financial year, and financial statements material for seven years. An electronic archive is acceptable as long as the data remains unaltered and accessible throughout. Final responsibility for retention obligations lies with you and your accountant, even if the system handles archiving automatically.

Does RAHTIS replace accounting software?

No. RAHTIS handles a job from order to archiving: the order, consignment note details, driver sign-offs, POD attachments, the invoice and payment tracking. The balance sheet and annual accounts are still handled by your accountant or in accounting software, which gets the data directly from the system. So your accountant still needs their own tools, but no longer scraps of paper about jobs.

Do freight exchange jobs fit into the same invoicing process?
