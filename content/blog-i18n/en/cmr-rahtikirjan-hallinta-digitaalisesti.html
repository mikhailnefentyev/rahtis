What is a CMR consignment note and which of its fields are mandatory?

The CMR consignment note is the document for international road carriage that confirms the carrier has taken over the goods and that a contract of carriage exists. Its content is defined by the CMR Convention, and the same form is valid throughout the EU, whether on paper or electronic.

Key fields under the CMR Convention

The mandatory fields are: sender and consignee, place of loading and place of delivery, description of the goods, gross weight, carriage charges, delivery instructions and date of carriage. The time the driver takes over the goods and the driver's signature, along with the consignee's entries on delivery, complete the document. The text of the CMR Convention is available on the UNECE website, and the Additional Protocol permits an electronic version.

What the consignment note proves and what it doesn't

The CMR proves the goods were taken over and in what quantity, but it proves neither the quality of the goods nor who owns them. Damage and reservations are recorded in the reservations box, and they determine liability afterwards. That is why the fields are best filled in carefully at loading, not afterwards from a spreadsheet. Is the electronic CMR (e-CMR) legal in Finland and the EU?

Yes. The electronic consignment note under the CMR Convention is governed by the 2008 Additional Protocol, which allows paper to be replaced by an electronic document provided the system guarantees the authenticity and integrity of the document and the identification of the signatory. Finland has ratified the Additional Protocol, so e-CMR is also legal for Finnish traffic and for cross-border journeys to other ratifying countries. In practice, both parties, the sender and the consignee, need to agree to the use of the electronic method.

The Additional Protocol and its requirements

The Additional Protocol does not prescribe any particular software; instead it sets requirements: the transport data must be tamper-proof, signatures must identify the signatory and carry a timestamp, and the data must remain available throughout the journey. If the system goes down or the data cannot be handed to the authorities at a roadside check, the method does not meet the requirements.

When to keep a paper copy as a fallback

When the customer or consignee doesn't use e-CMR, or when you're heading to a country that hasn't ratified the Additional Protocol, a paper CMR note is still the safest backup. Many small firms first use e-CMR domestically and keep paper forms in the cupboard for cross-border work. How is the consignment note signed digitally during the journey?

The driver's signature on a mobile device

The driver signs the CMR on a phone or tablet at loading. In practice an electronic signature means a fingerprint, a PIN code or a signature drawn on the screen, tied to the driver's ID and a timestamp. Finland has ratified the e-CMR Additional Protocol, so an electronic signature is as valid in court as a handwritten one Finland's ratification details for the e-CMR Additional Protocol (Finlex).

The consignee's signature and the moment of delivery

The consignee signs on the same device or on their own device, to which the driver sends a signature request. The moment of delivery is recorded in the system to the second, which settles disputes about when the goods were delivered. If the consignee refuses to sign electronically, the driver can revert to paper or record the discrepancy directly in the system.

What the signature must include

The signatory's name, role (sender, carrier or consignee), time and location are stored with the signature. This makes it possible to prove afterwards who signed and when. In RAHTIS, signature events and their details are available through the API, so they can be linked directly to invoicing and job status tracking (https://www.rahtis.eu/fi/api-docs). The signature does not replace reservations, but it locks them into the document unchanged.

How are CMR reservations (damage, discrepancies) recorded digitally?

Box 18: reservations and recording them electronically

Box 18 is the CMR reservations box: it records anything concerning the condition of the goods, the packaging or the delivery at the time of taking over. With an electronic CMR the entry is made directly in the system during the journey, and it is timestamped. In RAHTIS, reservations are recorded from the job's side panel, so the entry stays attached to that job rather than in a paper notebook.

When the entry must be made before signing

The reservation is recorded before the consignee signs the consignment note. An entry added after signing loses its evidential value, because the consignee has not seen or confirmed it. In an electronic system this boils down to one practical rule: inspect the load, record the reservations and photos, and only then sign. How long must a digital CMR be kept and how should it be archived?

Retention periods in Finland and the EU

In Finland, accounting records, which include consignment notes and transport documents as vouchers, must be kept for at least seven years under the Accounting Act (Finlex, Accounting Act 1336/1997). In the other Nordic and EU countries retention periods typically range from six to ten years, so always check the requirement in the customer's country before deciding how to archive. An electronic CMR is kept for the same number of years as a paper one.

Archiving: PDF, original data or both

A PDF copy on its own is the weakest option: its signature data cannot be verified later. It is safer to keep the original electronic document in a system that keeps the metadata and signatures unaltered. Many hauliers do both: the system holds the data, and PDFs are saved at year end as a backup.

In practice, archiving is best linked to the same place as dispatch and invoicing, so that seven years' worth of records don't end up scattered across different services. What should you check before switching from paper to digital consignment notes?

Choosing a system from a small firm's point of view

Test the system on one of your own empty runs and jobs before committing: how many clicks does it take to create a consignment note, and does it work on a phone at the depot without a network connection? Ask for a written price per company, not per user – in a 1–12 vehicle firm, per-user pricing quickly doubles the cost. Make sure the system supports CMR reservations and archiving as standard, not as an add-on module.

Training drivers and customers

For drivers, one practice run is enough: signing, attaching a photo and recording a discrepancy. Tell customers and consignees in advance that acknowledgement will be by link or app, and keep the paper option available for the first few weeks. Start with one regular route, not all jobs at once.

How does the digital consignment note link to dispatch and invoicing?

When the consignment note is digital, the same data can live throughout the job: order, dispatch, receipt and POD form a single chain that dispatch, the driver and invoicing all look at simultaneously. A paper CMR requires copying and re-keying at every stage, and errors arise precisely at those handovers.

The consignment note as part of the job life cycle

In practice, a job moves through statuses: published, assigned, collected, delivered, POD received, invoiced. When the consignment note is part of the system, the arrival of the POD can trigger the invoice directly, and an available drop trailer or return load shows up straight away without phone calls. The electronic signature is a precondition here, because the driver's and consignee's confirmation reaches the system immediately rather than waiting for the paper to come back to the office. The eIDAS Regulation provides a common EU framework for electronic signatures, on which confirmations between systems can be relied upon (EUR-Lex, eIDAS Regulation).

Documenting drop trailer and freight exchange jobs

On jobs taken from a freight exchange, the order, consignment note and POD are often in three different places. A digital chain keeps them in the same work queue, so documents can be traced even through a subcontracting chain. Summary: moving from paper to digital CMR

Break the switch into five steps. First, make sure the system you choose supports the e-CMR protocol and the mandatory fields. Second, try the signature on a single job before a wider roll-out. Third, make reservations part of the driver's routine so that damage and discrepancies are recorded during the journey. Fourth, sort out archiving: the digital CMR is kept in the system, but backups and the retention period are your responsibility. Fifth, link the consignment note to dispatch and invoicing, and the paper shuffle disappears altogether.

FAQ

Can a driver sign a CMR without a network connection or a smartphone?

Most systems support an offline mode: the signature and entries are stored on the device and synchronised later. If the driver has no smartphone at all, the options are a paper CMR note or confirming the driver's ID on an office device. Test the offline function on your own route before committing, because the signal often drops at depots and ports.

Will customs or an insurer accept a digital CMR instead of a paper one in a dispute?

Yes, if the system meets the requirements of the Additional Protocol: the document is tamper-proof, the signatures show the signatory and a timestamp, and the data can be handed over at an inspection. In a dispute, what decides the matter are the reservations, the photos and the to-the-second timestamps that e-CMR records automatically. Insurers value photos and a precise time of delivery because they shorten the investigation.

What do I do if the consignee refuses to sign electronically and insists on paper?

Go back to the paper form for that delivery and record the discrepancy in the system so the chain stays traceable. Tell the customer in advance that acknowledgement will be by link or app, and keep the paper option available for the first few weeks. Start with one regular route so that briefing consignees stays manageable and discrepancies don't pile up.

Can one consignment note cover a trailer swap or a drop trailer job with several vehicles?

A CMR is tied to a single contract of carriage, but changing tractor units doesn't prevent that: a change of driver or trailer is recorded as an entry on the same document. On a drop trailer job with several vehicles, each leg needs its own receipt and signature. Check how your system handles multi-leg journeys before bringing such jobs into the digital chain.
