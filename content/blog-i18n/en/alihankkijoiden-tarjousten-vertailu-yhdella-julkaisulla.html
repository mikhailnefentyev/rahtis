Key takeaways

  • Always include the same details in a request for quotation: route, vehicle requirement, schedule, load details, pricing basis and payment terms.
  • Send the same numbered list of questions to every subcontractor, so the replies can be read side by side.
  • Treat missing information as a risk: ask for it to be supplied before comparing, and set a deadline after which incomplete quotes are dropped.
  • Compare the total cost, not just the base price: waiting time charges, surcharges and payment terms change the ranking.
  • Keep the request, the replies and the comparison table in one place, so you can justify your choice later.

Put the same mandatory details in every request for quotation (route, weight, vehicle requirement, schedule, payment terms) and ask every subcontractor to reply in the same format. When the quotes are lined up side by side using the same fields, a single posting gives you a comparable overall picture without an email muddle. This article gives you a ready-made list of fields and a column structure for a comparison table that you can adopt for your own use.

What should a request for quotation contain to make the replies comparable?

A comparison falls apart if every subcontractor answers a different question. A request for quotation must include at least the route, the vehicle requirement, the timing, the load details, the pricing basis and the reply deadline. With these in order, you get back figures you can compare.

Route and timing in a consistent format

Record the origin and destination the same way every time: postcode, town and country code. Split the schedule into two fields, the collection window and the delivery window. Just "Helsinki → Turku" is not enough, because it does not tell you whether collection is in the city centre or at Vuosaari Harbour.

So record, for example, "00100 Helsinki, FI → 20100 Turku, FI", collection window "Fri 14 March 08:00–10:00" and delivery window "Fri 14 March 12:00–14:00". When everyone sees the same route definition, the prices are based on the same job rather than on different assumptions about the journey or driving restrictions.

Vehicle and driver requirements

State the vehicle type, payload and any special requirements, such as an ADR driver certificate or temperature-controlled equipment. If a requirement is missing, the cheapest quote may be one you cannot use. Also state whether loading, securing or unloading is the subcontractor's job. Specify the requirement in figures: "semi-trailer, curtainsider, 13.6 m, 33 euro pallet spaces, payload 24 t" is comparable; "big lorry" is not. If the load requires temperature control, state the target temperature and the permitted range. For dangerous goods, give the UN number and the ADR class, because the requirements depend on the class.

Pricing basis: fixed rate or hourly rate

Ask for a fixed rate for the route and a separate hourly rate for waiting time. Mixed prices are not comparable. Also list any surcharges separately, such as port and customs clearance charges and abnormal load permits. If one subcontractor includes them in the price and another invoices them separately, the comparison is skewed. So ask everyone for both a total price and a breakdown of extra charges.

How do you standardise the questions put to subcontractors?

The same list of questions for everyone. When every subcontractor answers a numbered list in the same order, the replies can be read side by side without interpretation.

A list of questions repeated for every subcontractor

The list is short and numbered. At least these points, in this order:

  1. Price excluding surcharges (€/transport or €/km)
  2. Collection and delivery times, to the nearest hour
  3. Vehicle type and payload
  4. Division of loading and unloading work
  5. Insurance and liability limits
  6. Cancellation terms and waiting time charges
  7. Invoicing details and payment period

Points 1–3 decide the comparison; points 4–7 prevent surprises later. When the questions are the same, "cheaper" means the same thing for everyone. Do not add points to the list "just in case": every extra question slows down replies and reduces the number of responses. You can ask the chosen subcontractor for further details after the comparison.

How to handle missing answers

Do not guess missing information; ask for it before comparing. If a subcontractor does not answer point 6, treat it as a risk, not as zero. Set a reply deadline (for example 24 hours), after which incomplete quotes are dropped from the table. That keeps the comparison fair.

If point 1, the price, is missing, the quote cannot be compared at all. If point 5, insurance, is missing, you can allow it to be supplied later, but mark the row "insurance not checked". Do not fill a blank field with your own assumption, even if you know the subcontractor's past practice.

How do you line up quotes side by side?

When the request for quotation is standardised field by field, each reply forms its own column or row in the table, and the comparison points come straight from the fields in the request. You can keep the table in any tool, as long as the columns are the same every time. On the RAHTIS load board, quotes arrive in the same view, but the broader list of questions and the comparison described here are done using your own template.

Structure of the comparison table

The fields follow the same order as the request: route, vehicle, schedule, price, waiting time charge, payment terms, cancellation terms and deviations. Freight exchanges such as TIMOCOM, Trans.eu and Teleroute present postings using their own standard fields. The advantage of your own template is that your fields, not the exchange's standard fields, decide the comparison.

Marking deviations and the total cost

If a subcontractor's answer to a field departs from the request, for example offering different payment terms or a different collection window, the deviation is marked directly in that cell, not in a separate memo. If subcontractor B states a payment period of 14 days instead of the 30 days requested, enter "14 days (requested 30 days)" in the cell.

Calculate the total cost of each quote: base price + (estimated waiting hours × hourly rate) + other surcharges. If waiting typically comes to two hours, B costs €420 + 2 × €35 = €490 and A €450 + 2 × €25 = €500, so B is still cheaper. The gap closes at three hours of waiting (B €525, A €525), and with longer waiting A is cheaper. The table makes this visible before you choose.

Which criteria besides price should the comparison use?

Response time says something about how they operate

Record how quickly the subcontractor replied to the request for quotation. Response time gives a hint of how quickly they react mid-transport when a lorry breaks down or loading is delayed. If a subcontractor promises to reply within half an hour but only answers the request three hours later, enter that in the table's response time column. A single slow reply does not tell you much, but a recurring one does.

Liability, insurance and payment terms

Ask as standard whether the subcontractor has valid carrier's liability insurance, what its maximum cover and excess are, and who pays the excess in the event of a claim. Also record the payment period and any advance payments: a short payment period or an advance payment ties up your cash flow just as a higher price does.

Remember the liability limits. In international carriage, the carrier's liability under the CMR Convention is limited to 8.33 special drawing rights (SDR) per kilogram of gross weight of the goods, and in domestic carriage to €20 per kilogram under section 32 of the Finnish Road Transport Contracts Act (Road Transport Contracts Act 345/1979, Finlex). If the value of the load is higher, the carrier's insurance will not cover its full value. In that case, the consignor can declare the value of the goods in the consignment note against an agreed surcharge, or take out separate cargo insurance for the goods. This information is collected under point 5.

Empty running and backloads

A subcontractor who already has a backload on the route may be able to quote a lower price, because the run pays for them in both directions. What matters to you, however, is what you pay for the transport, so compare the total cost, not the subcontractor's own kilometres. On a 200 km transport, for example, A's €450 is €2.25/km and B's €420 is €2.10/km. Thanks to the backload, B's price is lower and the run is profitable for them, which is also a good predictor that they will keep their promises.

How do you document the comparison so the decision can be justified?

A decision only stands up to scrutiny if the request, the replies and the completed table can be found in the same place. If they are scattered across emails and sticky notes, you will have to justify your choice from memory.

What to save and where

Save three things: the original request for quotation as it was sent, every reply with its date, and the final comparison table. On the RAHTIS load board, the transport and the quotes received for it are in the same view, and when you accept a quote, the same transport carries on as tracking, documents and an invoice. If you also compare quotes received via TIMOCOM or Trans.eu, save them in the same record as the others.

If the customer asks a month later why you chose the more expensive quote, a vague memory will not convince anyone. If you open the comparison table and show the row "highest price, but the only one that confirmed a vehicle within 48 hours", the justification is ready at once. This matters all the more for a regular transport whose costs build up month after month.

A one-line justification for every choice

Next to the table, write one line for each decision: why you chose A and why B and C were dropped. The line must refer to fields in the table, not to general impressions. "B was more flexible" is not a justification, but "B offered a 12-hour cancellation period, 24 h requested, accepted" is. The same principle lies at the heart of quality management systems such as ISO 9001: decisions are based on recorded information, not memory.

When is a single posting enough, and when do you need a separate tender?

A one-off load or ongoing volume

A one-off load or a series lasting a few weeks can be handled with a single posting: you post the transport, compare the replies and choose. For ongoing weekly volumes or a framework agreement, a single posting is not enough, because prices and terms have to be agreed for a longer period. A practical cut-off: if you need the same route with the same equipment once a month or less often, post it separately each time. If you need it weekly or more often, put the contract out to tender, for example once a year, on written terms, and use individual postings for exceptional situations.

Whether a quote is binding, and how long it is valid

Under section 1 of the Finnish Contracts Act (228/1929), an offer binds the person making it unless the offer itself or established practice in the sector indicates otherwise. Many, however, mark their quote "without obligation" or give it a short validity period. So when the decision will only be made later, ask for a clear validity period on every quote and record it in its own column in the table: "valid 7 days", "valid 14 days" or "without obligation" (Contracts Act, Finlex). That way you can see at a glance whether there is time to negotiate or you must decide straight away.

How does this work in practice in RAHTIS, and where does it fall short?

Posting and quotes in the same view

In RAHTIS, a transport is posted once: route, timing, unit and weight. The posting is visible to approved hauliers in the area, and quotes from up to three hauliers arrive in the same view. You can also send the transport first to selected vehicles you know, or directly to a single vehicle. For each bidder you can see vehicle details, ratings, punctuality history and a mark showing that their documents under the Finnish Contractor's Liability Act have been checked.

When you accept a quote, the same posting carries on as a transport: step-by-step tracking, the consignment note, photos and proof of delivery build up on the same job, and the invoice status is visible in the same service. Posting a transport and receiving quotes are free of charge for the party placing the order, and the contract is made directly with the haulier.

Where RAHTIS does not replace a tender

To be honest, RAHTIS is not a tendering tool. It has no list of questions of its own for bidders and no automatic comparison table; quotes are compared on price and the bidder's details. Framework tenders to dozens of subcontractors, lengthy contract negotiations and weighted evaluation criteria are best handled with your own template. If your customer is a public body, its own purchases are subject to the Finnish Act on Public Procurement, which RAHTIS does not take into account.

Price for hauliers

For a haulier, the first vehicle is free, and each additional vehicle that has run jobs during the month costs €14.90 a month (excl. VAT). An idle vehicle costs nothing, and no percentage is charged on jobs. The first month is free (RAHTIS).

FAQ

How many subcontractors should I ask to quote for a single load?

Three or four is usually enough. With two quotes it is hard to get a sense of the market price, and with many bidders the new information you get is not worth the effort. Ask for a validity period on every quote, so the cheapest option does not disappear midway through the comparison.

Can I use the same template for a freight exchange posting too?

Yes. The template's fields, i.e. route, vehicle requirement, schedule, pricing basis and payment terms, complement the exchange's standard posting. TIMOCOM, Trans.eu and Teleroute present postings using their own standard fields, but your template decides what is compared in the replies.

What do I do if a subcontractor replies in free form and ignores the template?

Record the deviations directly in the relevant cells and ask for the missing points to be supplied before comparing. A missing point 6 (cancellation terms and waiting time charges) is a major risk. Set a reply deadline, for example 24 hours, after which incomplete quotes are dropped.

How much does RAHTIS cost a small haulier?

The first vehicle is free, and each additional vehicle that has run jobs during the month costs €14.90 a month excluding VAT. An idle vehicle costs nothing, and no percentage is charged on jobs. For example, a fleet of four active vehicles costs 3 × €14.90 = €44.70 a month (excl. VAT).
