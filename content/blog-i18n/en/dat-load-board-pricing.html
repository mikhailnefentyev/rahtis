DAT load board pricing runs from $59 per month for DAT One Standard to $339 for the Office carrier plan and $449 for the top broker plan, with extra users and add-on tools pushing the total higher. This breakdown covers each tier line by line, then compares the total cost against European freight exchanges like Teleroute, TIMOCOM and Trans.eu, so a Finland-based carrier running rahtipörssi loads can decide whether DAT is worth the budget at all.

How much does DAT load board cost per month in each tier?

DAT lists five carrier plans and three broker plans on its pricing page. At the time of writing (November 2026), the carrier plans cost $59 a month for DAT One Standard, $149 for Enhanced, $169 for Pro, $259 for Select and $339 for Office; the broker plans cost $159 for Express, $319 for Select Broker and $449 for Office Broker, and combined broker/carrier plans $339 or $469 (DAT One pricing). The page lists monthly prices only. Standard covers basic load search; Enhanced adds 15-day average lane rates; Pro adds 7-day lane rates, more search tabs and TriHaul routing; Select and Office show the best available lane rates, and Office adds workgroups for several users.

Published rates vs quoted rates

DAT's checkout price is a floor, not the final number. Sales regularly quotes different figures on the phone, and add-ons such as extra user seats or separate analytics products change the total. Treat any quote as negotiable and get it in writing before you commit.

Monthly vs annual billing math

DAT's pricing page lists monthly prices only and publishes no annual-billing discount; if sales offers a cheaper rate for a longer commitment, get it in writing, because it locks you in for the whole term. For a 1-12 truck operation testing whether DAT fits alongside your rahtipörssi logins, one month of Pro is the cheaper experiment; a longer commitment only pays off once you know you'll use it weekly. Whatever you sign, check how your data and documents are handled before uploading loads or CMR details, as with any platform (see, for example, how we document this at RAHTIS privacy).

What is the difference between DAT One Select, Pro, and Broker tiers?

DAT splits its carrier load board into five plans, and the gap between them is mostly about how many searches you can run side by side and how fresh the rate data is. The names change occasionally, but the structure holds: cheap entry plans, mid plans for owner-operators who live on the board, and top plans for fleets, with separate plans for brokers.

What the entry plans limit

Standard and Enhanced are the floor. Standard is aimed at partial-load operators and shows little rate data; Enhanced adds 15-day average lane rates but only two search tabs. That is workable if you run one truck and check the board once a morning. The moment you hunt return loads across several lanes or recheck rates mid-day, the limits bite, and with older rate averages you are negotiating partly blind.

When Pro pays for itself

Pro adds a third search tab, fresher 7-day lane rates and TriHaul routing, and Select moves up to the best available lane rates and ten search tabs; fresher rate data is the real reason carriers upgrade. If you subcontract from a freight exchange alongside your own clients, knowing the going rate on a lane before you quote is worth more than the tier difference. The same logic applies on this side of the Atlantic: TIMOCOM and Trans.eu both charge for rate and market data at higher access levels, so the pattern is not unique to DAT. A 4-truck outfit where one person dispatches, invoices and chases PODs should judge any tier by one question: does it cut a phone call or a notebook entry per keikka? That is also the test we built RAHTIS around, publishing a job once and following it to paid invoice. Where DAT falls short for Nordic operators is lane coverage: it is strongest in North America, thin on Finnish domestic and Baltic cross-border freight.

Broker and Office plan scope

The broker plans (Express, Select Broker, Office Broker) are for posting loads and finding carriers, with DAT's market-rate data; Office adds workgroups for several users. For a 1-12 truck carrier the broker plans are overkill; you are paying for features that only matter if you broker other carriers' freight.

Are there hidden fees like setup, add-on, or cancellation charges?

The headline monthly price is not the whole bill. Extra users cost extra (on the Select and Office plans DAT lists $169 a month per additional user), and DAT sells separate products such as RateView analytics next to the load board. What each rep bundles varies, which is why two carriers on the same tier can pay different amounts. DAT's own sales pages list the add-on products separately from the core membership (DAT sales page), so ask for a line-item quote before signing.

Cancellation and auto-renewal terms

DAT One plans are billed monthly and renew automatically. Per DAT's support pages, a cancellation takes effect on the next billing date and unused time is not refunded, so cancel before the cycle ends. If sales sells you a longer term, its own notice and cancellation terms apply; get them in writing at signup.

Budget the same way you would for a rahtipörssi subscription: base fee, add-ons, and the commitment period. If you also run a TMS like RAHTIS alongside a freight exchange, count the DAT add-ons against what those tools already cover so you are not paying twice for rates and document handling.

Is DAT pricing different for owner-operators vs fleets vs brokers?

Per-seat vs per-company pricing

DAT prices by membership tier, not by truck count, so a one-truck owner-operator and a twelve-truck carrier on the same plan pay the same base subscription. The plan price covers one user, and the cost scales when you add users: a dispatcher who is also the accountant and safety guy needs one login, but a second dispatcher or an office clerk means another per-user charge. Brokers pay more because the Broker tier adds features carriers never touch, like posting loads for carriers rather than searching for loads.

Why the 4-truck owner pays disproportionately

If you dispatch alone, per-seat pricing is actually in your favour: one seat covers the whole operation. The disproportionate cost is your time, not the invoice. Juggling a load board, a separate TMS and a paper rahtikirja book is the real per-user fee, paid in evenings. That is the problem a combined freight exchange and TMS like RAHTIS targets, with one place to find keikka, dispatch and invoice, and no per-seat penalty for being a one-person office.

How does DAT pricing compare to European freight exchanges like Teleroute, TIMOCOM and Trans.eu?

Coverage vs cost for Northern European lanes

DAT is built around US and Canadian lanes. A Finnish carrier moving port, terminal or irtoperä freight out of Helsinki, Turku or Hanko will find almost nothing to bid on there, so the monthly fee buys access you cannot use. Teleroute, TIMOCOM and Trans.eu all operate on European corridors, which is where your keikka traffic actually lives. If you also pull backhaul from a Nordic rahtipörssi, a US load board adds a second login without adding a single return load. Check the renewal terms before subscribing to anything: DAT subscriptions auto-renew, and the DAT site is where you find the account and billing terms that govern cancellation.

Subcontracting and offer-tab workflows

The European exchanges are built for the subcontracting chain: you post a load, carriers bid in the offer tab, you accept, and the freight exchange documents the assignment. That maps directly to how a small Finnish fleet covers overflow with subcontracting partners. DAT's workflow assumes you are the one searching for trucks, not handing loads to subcontractors, so its tier structure rewards a different job than yours.

Is DAT worth the price for a small carrier, or should the budget go to a TMS?

The stacked-tools cost trap

The subscription fee is only part of what a load board costs you. Add a separate TMS, a separate invoicing tool, and the hours you spend each week retyping the same load into three systems, and the real number is much higher. For a one-person dispatch office running 4-12 trucks, admin time is often the biggest line item, and it never shows up on a quote.

Can you negotiate or get a discount on DAT membership?

DAT does not publish discount rates, but the subscription is sold through a sales team, and reps routinely have room to move on price if you commit to a longer term or ask directly. Committing to a longer term is the usual lever; month-to-month is the rate you pay for flexibility. What is fixed: the tier structure itself and the per-user add-ons, which rarely get discounted for a 1-12 truck operation.

European exchanges are no different here. Teleroute does not list prices either: its price depends on company size, number of users and the modules you need, and you get it after a demo (Teleroute membership). Whichever board you pick, compare written quotes, not list prices.

Which DAT tier fits which carrier profile?

If you run one truck and book everything from your phone, the entry tier covers the load board itself; you don't need broker features or extra seats. A 2-6 truck fleet with own clients plus freight exchange subcontracting needs fresh rate data and several search tabs, so Pro or Select is the honest fit; pay for an extra user only if a second person dispatches.

If you also broker loads or post freight for subcontractors, a broker or combined broker/carrier plan is unavoidable, since posting loads is broker-side functionality. But a Nordic carrier subcontracting via Teleroute, TIMOCOM or Trans.eu should ask a harder question first: does DAT access pay for itself at all, or is the budget better spent on dispatch and invoicing that runs the trucks you already have?

FAQ

Does DAT charge per user or per company?

DAT charges by plan, not by truck: the monthly plan price covers one user, so a one-truck owner-operator and a twelve-truck carrier on the same plan pay the same base price. The cost scales with users, not trucks. A solo dispatcher who also handles invoicing needs one login, but adding a second dispatcher or office clerk means paying for another user (DAT lists $169 a month per additional user on Select and Office).

Can I use DAT load board from Finland or elsewhere in Europe?

Yes, you can subscribe and search from Finland, but the value is limited. DAT's lane coverage is strongest in North America and thin on Finnish domestic and Baltic cross-border freight, so a carrier running Helsinki, Turku or Hanko traffic will find little to bid on. European exchanges like Teleroute, TIMOCOM and Trans.eu cover those corridors instead.

What happens to my posted loads and rate data if I cancel DAT?

DAT One plans are billed monthly and renew automatically; per DAT's support pages, a cancellation takes effect on the next billing date, with no refund for unused time. Posted loads and searches end with access, so download anything you need before the billing period closes. If you signed a longer term with sales, check its cancellation terms.

Is there a free trial before paying for a DAT tier?

DAT's pricing page offers no free trial of the paid plans; a free DAT One account gives only limited access. The practical alternative is one month of a mid tier paid monthly: it tests whether the board fits your lanes without locking you into a long commitment while you evaluate it.

Do I still need a separate TMS if I have a DAT subscription?

Usually yes. DAT is a load board with rate data; it does not dispatch, invoice or track your paperwork, so most carriers stack a TMS or invoicing tool on top, retyping loads between systems. A combined freight exchange and TMS like RAHTIS covers find-load-to-paid-invoice in one place, though it does not replace DAT for North American customers.
