An HGV driver's pay isn't a single figure but a sum: basic pay from the collective agreement (TES) pay table, an experience supplement, shift and overtime supplements, and taxable allowances. The collective agreement and the employer's own contract determine what a driver can earn, and Posti has its own table. This article unpicks the structure and works out an example of monthly net pay.

What is the average pay for an HGV driver in Finland?

An HGV driver in Finland typically earns around €3,000–3,800 a month before tax. The exact figure depends on experience, routes driven and supplements, but the baseline is set by the road transport collective agreement (TES), whose pay tables set drivers' minimum wages in full.

When you're pricing jobs or hiring a driver, the collective agreement table is the figure you can't go below. Everything above it is up to you: experience supplements, night and Sunday work, and per-kilometre or per-route payments quickly push the total up.

The same logic applies to earning from your own work. If you count trips, supplements and unpaid invoices by hand, any review of pay levels is only ever half done. RAHTIS (rahtis.eu/fi) keeps the voucher, consignment note and invoice in one chain, so you know what each shift actually brought in.

How is an HGV driver's pay set under the collective agreement?

An HGV driver's pay isn't at the employer's whim: the sector's generally binding collective agreement (TES) sets minimum wages, and no lower monthly wage may be agreed. If you run a firm with 1–12 vehicles, the pay table is the base to which you add experience pay, supplements and allowances.

What does the collective agreement pay table contain?

The pay table groups drivers by years of experience: the longer they've been in the trade, the higher the pay band they move up to. The table gives the minimum monthly wage, and alongside it there are experience-based pay rates, the principles of time-based and performance-based pay, and the terms of the employment contract. Below is an example of the structure; the figures are illustrative and do not replace the table currently in force:

Always take the exact figures from the collective agreement table currently in force, as they are updated with each agreement period. The table also covers separate provisions for drivers with a specialist vocational qualification and for transport using vehicle combinations over 12 tonnes. These factors raise the minimum wage automatically without separate negotiation.

How does the collective agreement bind the employer?

The collective agreement is binding: an employment contract may not set terms that are worse than the agreement, even if the driver agrees to them. The minimum wage is a floor, not a ceiling, so market pressure often pushes pay above it. If you employ drivers or are looking for work elsewhere, RAHTIS helps keep jobs and their profitability in order alongside pay: more information and the application form are at https://www.rahtis.eu/en/apply.

In practice, general applicability means that even an employer who isn't a member of the employers' federation has to comply with the terms of the collective agreement, provided the work done falls within the agreement's scope. You can check this on the website of SKAL (the Finnish Transport and Logistics association), which has up-to-date information on the agreements in force and their interpretation. If you don't want to risk disputes over interpretation, keep pay above the collective agreement table.

How much do experience and further training raise a driver's pay?

The collective agreement pay tables are built around years of experience: pay rises in steps as the driver's experience in the trade grows. In practice, a few years' experience moves a driver from the lower pay bands to the higher ones, and the effect shows directly in the hourly rate. According to the wage statistics of Statistics Finland, more experienced drivers' average earnings are clearly higher than the sector average (Statistics Finland wage statistics).

Additional certificates work in a different way. An ADR certificate for carrying dangerous goods, a qualification for operating loading equipment and add-on modules to a category C licence make a driver fit for work that would otherwise go unfilled. Many small operators pay extra for such work through the collective agreement supplements, so the certificate pays off for both driver and employer.

For an owner-driver, experience shows up through a different channel: as a seasoned professional you get more loads done and judge routes and return loads better. If you're planning to hire your first driver, put the experience requirement and additional certificates in the job advert and direct applicants to a detailed description of the work, for example via a freight platform.

Years of experience don't accrue automatically: the driver must be able to prove previous work history with payslips or employment certificates. The collective agreement also distinguishes between domestic and foreign experience, and experience driving abroad doesn't necessarily transfer directly into a pay band under the Finnish table. Bear this in mind if you're hiring experienced drivers from the Baltics or elsewhere abroad.

How much does Posti pay an HGV driver?

Posti follows its own collective agreement, which differs from the general road haulage collective agreement. In practice, this means a Posti driver's pay is set by Posti's own pay tables, and experience and area of work affect pay differently than for an ordinary haulier.

As a large employer, Posti also has its own supplement models: shift supplements, Sunday work enhancements and route-specific arrangements are part of its own agreement. So its pay doesn't tell you directly what you should pay your own driver.

When comparing pay, compare the whole package, not just basic pay. Work out your own pay level from your own collective agreement supplements and working hours, while making sure jobs and invoicing run without manual paperwork. RAHTIS helps keep track of who does what and who pays what, on our Latvian pages too (https://www.rahtis.eu/lv).

A particular feature of Posti's pay is the transparency and predictability of its supplements, whereas as a small operator you may have to negotiate each extra item separately. That can be an advantage when competing for staff, if you can offer a clear model in which the driver knows exactly what each route and shift will bring. A small operator's flexibility in agreeing terms can also work in your favour, as the pay structure can be tailored around that particular driver's strengths.

Which supplements and allowances make up an HGV driver's pay?

An HGV driver's pay isn't just the monthly salary: it includes supplements, daily allowances and mileage allowances, which can raise gross pay considerably. When you calculate a driver's pay yourself or compare costs when pricing a job, it's worth itemising these components separately.

How are supplements calculated?

Supplements are usually calculated as a percentage of basic pay or the hourly rate. Night work supplements, Sunday and public holiday supplements and overtime pay are set by the sector's collective agreement, and their size depends on when the work is done. The experience supplement and supplements for additional training also go into the same gross pay. In practice, this means two drivers on the same basic pay can receive different amounts each month if one of them drives nights and weekends.

The employer must keep records of all supplements and allowances so that they can be checked when the annual accounts are prepared and in any dispute over interpreting the collective agreement. Daily allowances paid for international driving must be itemised separately, because their tax treatment differs from ordinary pay. The Finnish Tax Administration records these allowances as a separate sum, and they must not be hidden in the general pay total.

What is paid on international work?

On international work, pay is topped up with a daily allowance, paid according to the number of hours for each day spent abroad, plus any supplements for cross-border work. Part of the daily allowance is tax-free, so it is a favourable component of pay in net terms. The collective agreement of Posti and its partners has its own provisions on drivers' supplements and allowances, which are worth checking directly in the collective agreement information published by Posti. When you invoice your own work or subcontracting, the RAHTIS API specifications help you feed job data and allowances straight into your invoicing system, so the paper "who owes what" notebook becomes unnecessary.

On international work, a driver may also receive a separate border-crossing allowance, paid for each border crossed. In practice this can mean tens of euros extra a week if the route passes through several countries. Parking charges and road tolls are not usually part of the driver's pay, but they must be included in the employer's total costs when pricing international haulage.

How does HGV driver pay vary by region and by employer?

The biggest pay differences don't come from regional boundaries but from the employer's size and contractual basis. At large operators such as Posti, the collective agreement pay scale is usually the base, with company-specific benefits added on top, whereas in small firms pay is often negotiated directly around the collective agreement minimum.

Regionally, pay in Uusimaa and Pirkanmaa is typically higher than in eastern and northern Finland, but the difference is often smaller than the difference in housing costs. Urban multi-drop and refuse collection work pays more than long-haul work, where overnight allowances make up part of the difference.

When looking at employers, check three things: does the company pay the collective agreement's general supplements in full, is overtime paid or does it go unpaid, and how regularly does the monthly pay include a performance-related element. The same driver doing the same work at two different companies can see a difference of several hundred euros in monthly pay.

Competition for drivers is fiercest in growth centres, where employers have to raise pay to keep up. An experienced driver can negotiate a better package at a small firm where the employer can't replace them quickly. Besides the Helsinki region, pay in port towns such as Kotka and Hamina is often above the local average, because export and import traffic constantly needs new drivers.

How does HGV driver pay compare with other jobs in transport?

In Finland, an HGV driver's pay is typically close to that of a bus driver or delivery driver, while taxi drivers and van drivers often earn less. The differences are largely explained by the collective agreements: on the HGV side, years of experience and further training raise pay predictably, whereas in lighter distribution pay is often hourly without the same experience ladder.

A maintenance worker or warehouse operative usually earns less than an experienced HGV driver, but office roles such as transport manager or invoicing specialist can exceed a driver's basic pay. The driver's advantage lies in supplements and allowances, which can lift total pay well above the basic hourly rate.

When comparing roles, always calculate net pay in the same way. The Finnish Tax Administration's salary calculator gives a comparable figure for different gross salaries: the Tax Administration salary calculator.

The comparison should also take account of the physical strain and irregular working hours typical of HGV driving. A delivery driver may get the same gross pay, but the work may be more physically demanding and involve more contact with customers. A long-haul driver, on the other hand, spends weeks away from home, which affects net pay once you take daily allowances and any other tax-free allowances into account. Comparing occupations is therefore multi-dimensional, and the monthly total alone doesn't tell the whole story.

Worked example: a driver's monthly gross and net pay

Take a hypothetical driver with five years' experience and basic haulage pay under the collective agreement table. Say the monthly salary is €3,200. On top of that comes performance pay, for example 300 kilometres per shift at €0.10, i.e. €30, plus 15 hours of overtime at the enhanced rate. Supplements and allowances bring gross pay to around €3,500.

Deducted from gross pay are the employee pension contribution (7.15% in 2025), the unemployment insurance contribution and tax according to the tax card. A rough rule of thumb: €3,500 gross comes to about €2,700–2,850 net, depending on the tax rate and deductions.

The cost to the employer is higher: pension contributions, health insurance and holiday pay add roughly 20–25% on top of gross pay, so a salary of €3,500 costs the employer about €4,300.

The calculation is indicative. The exact figures depend on the agreement's pay table, the amount of supplements and the driver's tax rate, so do your own calculation with your own figures before submitting a quote.

If the driver regularly works weekends, the Sunday supplement can raise monthly pay significantly. Under the collective agreement, Sunday work is paid at a 100% enhancement on top of the hourly rate, so a 10-hour Sunday shift adds about €150–200 gross, depending on the basic hourly rate. Similarly, the night work supplement, paid for hours between 22:00 and 06:00, can add 20–30%, which makes long night shifts a considerable boost to pay. When you're calculating pay for a quote, ask the driver how many weekend and night shifts they're prepared to do, so you can arrive at a realistic gross figure.

FAQ

Does an HGV driver have guaranteed pay if there is less work?

Yes, a monthly paid driver has guaranteed pay under the collective agreement, which is paid even when fewer jobs come in. The table's minimum wage is a floor below which pay cannot be agreed, even if the amount of work varies from week to week. Hourly paid arrangements follow different rules, so the type of employment contract is what decides.

How is a driver paid for overtime under the collective agreement?

Overtime is compensated with an enhancement under the collective agreement that increases with the number of overtime hours: a smaller supplement for the first hours, a larger one after that. The compensation is calculated by the hour and added to gross pay as taxable income. Monthly pay can rise noticeably if overtime builds up regularly: in the worked example, 15 hours of overtime raised gross pay.

Does the employer pay for a driver's Driver CPC training?

Initial and periodic Driver CPC training are usually paid for by the employer, since they are a prerequisite for the job. Additional certificates obtained by the driver, such as ADR or a loading equipment qualification, depend on the agreement. Many employers pay for them or compensate them through collective agreement supplements, because they make the driver more versatile.

Does pay rise if the driver also drives demanding vehicles or does ADR work?

Often, but not automatically. An ADR certificate and the ability to drive more demanding vehicles make the driver fit for work that would otherwise go unfilled, so market pressure and collective agreement supplements push pay up. Years of experience move the driver into higher pay bands, and extra allowances are usually paid separately for demanding runs.
