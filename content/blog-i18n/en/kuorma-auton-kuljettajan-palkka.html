An HGV driver's pay isn't a single figure but a sum: basic pay from the collective agreement (TES) pay table, evening, night and overtime supplements, and tax-free daily allowances. The collective agreement and the employment contract determine what a driver can earn, and Posti is covered by a different collective agreement. This article unpicks the structure and works out an example of monthly net pay.

What is the average pay for an HGV driver in Finland?

An HGV driver in Finland typically earns around €3,000–3,800 a month before tax. The exact figure depends on experience, routes driven and supplements, but the baseline is set by the HGV sector collective agreement (TES) between the Finnish Road Transport Employers' Federation (ATL) and the Finnish Transport Workers' Union AKT, whose pay tables set drivers' minimum wages.

When you're pricing jobs or hiring a driver, the collective agreement table is the figure you can't go below. Everything above it is up to you: experience supplements, night and Sunday work, and per-kilometre or per-route payments quickly push the total up.

The same logic applies to earning from your own work. If you count trips, supplements and unpaid invoices by hand, any review of pay levels is only ever half done. RAHTIS (rahtis.eu/fi) keeps the job, consignment note and invoice in one chain, so you know what each shift actually brought in.

How is an HGV driver's pay set under the collective agreement?

An HGV driver's pay isn't at the employer's whim: the sector's generally binding collective agreement (TES) sets minimum wages, and no lower wage may be agreed. If you run a firm with 1–12 vehicles, the pay table is the base to which you add experience pay, supplements and allowances.

What does the collective agreement pay table contain?

The pay table first groups drivers by vehicle (full-trailer and modular combinations, semi-trailers, rigid trucks, vans) and then, within each group, by years of service: under 4, 4–8, 8–12 and over 12 years. Pay is an hourly rate or a two-week period wage (80 hours), not a monthly salary. Below are the table rates for a semi-trailer (CE) driver from 1 June 2026; the monthly level is worked out from the period wage:

Always check the exact figures in the collective agreement table currently in force, as they are updated during the agreement period (next on 1 June 2027). International haulage has its own table, and a vocational qualification counts as eight months of service. In addition, drivers of HCT combinations, crane trucks and low-loaders, for example, get a 5% special supplement on the table rate without separate negotiation.

How does the collective agreement bind the employer?

The collective agreement is binding: an employment contract may not set terms that are worse than the agreement, even if the driver agrees to them. The minimum wage is a floor, not a ceiling, so market pressure often pushes pay above it. If you employ drivers, RAHTIS helps keep jobs and their profitability in order alongside pay: more information and the application form are at https://www.rahtis.eu/en/apply.

In practice, general applicability means that even an employer who isn't a member of the employers' federation has to comply with the terms of the collective agreement, provided the work done falls within the agreement's scope. You can check this on the website of SKAL (the Finnish Transport and Logistics association), which has up-to-date information on the agreements in force and their interpretation. If you don't want to risk disputes over interpretation, keep pay above the collective agreement table.

How much do experience and further training raise a driver's pay?

The collective agreement pay tables step up with years of service: pay rises as the driver's experience in the trade grows. In practice, at four, eight and twelve years the driver moves up to a higher pay row, and the effect shows directly in the hourly rate. According to the wage statistics of Statistics Finland, more experienced drivers' average earnings are clearly higher than the sector average (Statistics Finland wage statistics).

Additional certificates work in a different way. An ADR certificate for carrying dangerous goods, a crane or loader qualification and a category CE licence make a driver fit for work that would otherwise go unfilled. The collective agreement pays separate supplements for such work, for example 5% of the table rate for ADR transport of explosives, toxic, infectious and radioactive substances, so the certificate pays off for both driver and employer.

For an owner-driver, experience shows up through a different channel: as a seasoned professional you get more loads done and judge routes and return loads better. If you're planning to hire your first driver, put the experience requirement and additional certificates in the job advert and direct applicants to a detailed description of the work, for example via a freight platform.

Years of service don't accrue automatically: the driver must be able to prove previous work history with employment certificates. The collective agreement doesn't distinguish between domestic and foreign experience: all professional driving shown in employment certificates counts as service. So if you're hiring experienced drivers from the Baltics or elsewhere abroad, ask for their employment certificates at the application stage.

How much does Posti pay an HGV driver?

Posti follows the communications and logistics sector collective agreement between the Service Sector Employers Palta and the Post and Logistics Union PAU, together with its protocol on Posti, not the HGV sector agreement. In practice, this means a Posti driver's pay is set by that agreement's pay tables, and experience and area of work affect pay differently than for an ordinary haulier.

As a large employer, Posti also has its own supplement models: shift supplements, Sunday work enhancements and route-specific arrangements are set by its own agreement. So its pay doesn't tell you directly what you should pay your own driver.

When comparing pay, compare the whole package, not just basic pay. Work out your own pay level from your own collective agreement supplements and working hours, while making sure jobs and invoicing run without manual paperwork. RAHTIS helps keep track of who does what and who pays what, on our Latvian pages too (https://www.rahtis.eu/lv).

A particular feature of Posti's pay is the transparency and predictability of its supplements, whereas as a small operator you may have to negotiate each extra item separately. That can be an advantage when competing for staff, if you can offer a clear model in which the driver knows exactly what each route and shift will bring. A small operator's flexibility in agreeing terms can also work in your favour, as the pay structure can be tailored around that particular driver's strengths.

Which supplements and allowances make up an HGV driver's pay?

An HGV driver's pay isn't just the monthly salary: it includes supplements, daily allowances and mileage allowances, which can raise gross pay considerably. When you calculate a driver's pay yourself or compare costs when pricing a job, it's worth itemising these components separately.

How are supplements calculated?

Supplements are calculated as a percentage of the table rate or of average hourly earnings. Under the HGV collective agreement, the evening supplement (18:00–22:00) is 15% and the night supplement (22:00–06:00) 20% of the table rate, Sunday and public holiday work is paid at a 100% enhancement, and overtime pay is set by the agreement. Special supplements and the international haulage supplement also go into the same gross pay. In practice, this means two drivers on the same basic pay can receive different amounts each month if one of them drives nights and weekends.

The employer must keep records of all supplements and allowances so that they can be checked in payroll, in taxation and in any dispute over interpreting the collective agreement. Daily allowances and other travel expense allowances must be itemised separately, because up to the Finnish Tax Administration's maximum amounts they are tax-free, unlike pay. They are reported to the Incomes Register as a separate item and must not be hidden in the general pay total.

What is paid on international work?

On international work, the collective agreement's international haulage pay table applies, and a driver who has worked in international haulage at the same company for at least two years gets a special supplement of at least 8%. On top of that comes a foreign daily allowance according to the length of the trip. The daily allowance is tax-free up to the maximum set by the Finnish Tax Administration, so it is a favourable component of earnings in net terms. Supplements and allowances for drivers at Posti and its partners are set by the communications and logistics sector collective agreement, so check them with Posti or the unions that signed the agreement. When you invoice your own work or subcontracting, the RAHTIS API specifications help you feed job data and allowances straight into your invoicing system, so the paper notebook of who owes what becomes unnecessary.

The HGV collective agreement has no separate border-crossing allowance; if an employer pays one, it has been agreed in the employment contract and is taxable pay. Parking charges and road tolls are not usually part of the driver's pay, but they must be included in the employer's total costs when pricing international haulage.

How does HGV driver pay vary by region and by employer?

The biggest pay differences don't come from regional boundaries but from the employer's size and contractual basis. At large operators such as Posti, the collective agreement pay scale is usually the base, with company-specific benefits added on top, whereas in small firms pay is often negotiated directly around the collective agreement minimum.

Regionally, pay in Uusimaa and Pirkanmaa is typically higher than in eastern and northern Finland, but the difference is often smaller than the difference in housing costs. Urban multi-drop and refuse collection work can pay more than long-haul work, where daily allowances make up part of the difference.

When looking at employers, check three things: does the company pay the collective agreement's general supplements in full, is overtime paid or does it go unpaid, and how regularly does the monthly pay include a performance-related element. The same driver doing the same work at two different companies can see a difference of several hundred euros in monthly pay.

Competition for drivers is fiercest in growth centres, where employers have to raise pay to keep up. An experienced driver can negotiate a better package at a small firm where the employer can't replace them quickly. Besides the Helsinki region, pay in port towns such as Kotka and Hamina is often above the local average, because export and import traffic constantly needs new drivers.

How does HGV driver pay compare with other jobs in transport?

In Finland, an HGV driver's pay is typically close to that of a bus driver or delivery driver, while taxi drivers and van drivers often earn less. The differences are largely explained by the collective agreements: on the HGV side, years of experience and further training raise pay predictably, whereas in lighter distribution pay is often hourly without the same experience ladder.

A maintenance worker or warehouse operative usually earns less than an experienced HGV driver, but office roles such as transport manager or invoicing specialist can exceed a driver's basic pay. The driver's advantage lies in supplements and allowances, which can lift total pay well above the basic hourly rate.

When comparing roles, always calculate net pay in the same way. The Finnish Tax Administration's tax card calculator (vero.fi) gives a comparable tax rate for different gross salaries.

The comparison should also take account of the physical strain and irregular working hours typical of HGV driving. A delivery driver may get the same gross pay, but the work may be more physically demanding and involve more contact with customers. A long-haul driver, on the other hand, spends weeks away from home, which affects net pay once you take daily allowances and any other tax-free allowances into account. Comparing occupations is therefore multi-dimensional, and the monthly total alone doesn't tell the whole story.

Worked example: a driver's monthly gross and net pay

Take a hypothetical semi-trailer driver with five years' experience. The minimum under the collective agreement table is about €2,900 a month for them, but the employment contract sets €3,200. On top of that comes performance pay, for example 300 kilometres at €0.10 per kilometre, i.e. €30, plus 10 hours of overtime at a 50% enhancement, about €280. Supplements and allowances bring gross pay to around €3,500.

Deducted from gross pay are the employee pension contribution (7.30% in 2026 for all ages), the unemployment insurance contribution (0.89%), the health insurance daily allowance contribution (0.88%) and tax withheld according to the tax card. A rough rule of thumb: €3,500 gross comes to about €2,550–2,650 net, depending on the municipality, church tax and deductions.

The cost to the employer is higher: the employer's share of the pension contribution, the health insurance contribution and unemployment, accident and group life insurance add roughly 20–25% on top of gross pay, so a salary of €3,500 costs the employer about €4,300.

The calculation is indicative. The exact figures depend on the agreement's pay table, the amount of supplements and the driver's tax rate, so do your own calculation with your own figures before submitting a quote.

If the driver regularly works weekends, the Sunday supplement can raise monthly pay significantly. Under the collective agreement, Sunday work is paid at a 100% enhancement, so a 10-hour Sunday shift adds about €170–200 gross, depending on average hourly earnings. Similarly, the night work supplement, paid for hours between 22:00 and 06:00, adds 20% to the table rate, which makes long night shifts a considerable boost to pay. When you're calculating pay for a quote, ask the driver how many weekend and night shifts they're prepared to do, so you can arrive at a realistic gross figure.

FAQ

Does an HGV driver have guaranteed pay if there is less work?

Yes, a driver on a period wage has guaranteed pay under the collective agreement: if working hours in a two-week period fall below those agreed in the employment contract for a reason on the employer's side, pay is still paid up to the agreed number of hours. The table rate is a floor below which pay cannot be agreed, even if the amount of work varies. Part-time and hourly paid arrangements follow different rules, so the type of employment contract is what decides.

How is a driver paid for overtime under the collective agreement?

Overtime is compensated with an enhancement under the collective agreement that increases with the number of overtime hours: 50% for the first 12 hours over the 80 hours of a two-week period, and 100% after that. The enhancement is calculated from average hourly earnings and added to gross pay as taxable income. Monthly pay can rise noticeably if overtime builds up regularly: in the worked example, 10 hours of overtime raised gross pay by about €280.

Does the employer pay for a driver's Driver CPC training?

Under the HGV collective agreement, the employer pays the cost of obtaining and renewing the certificates and cards required in the job, such as the ADR certificate, the cost of the Driver CPC entry in the licence after periodic training, and the driver card, if the driver needs them in the job. The initial Driver CPC is usually obtained before employment. If employment ends for a reason on the driver's side soon after the purchase, the driver may have to repay part of the cost.

Does pay rise if the driver also drives demanding vehicles or does ADR work?

Often, but not automatically. An ADR certificate and the ability to drive more demanding vehicles make the driver fit for work that would otherwise go unfilled, so market pressure and collective agreement supplements push pay up. A heavier combination means a higher pay group, years of service move the driver into higher pay rows, and the agreement's 5% ADR and special supplements are paid separately for certain demanding transport.
