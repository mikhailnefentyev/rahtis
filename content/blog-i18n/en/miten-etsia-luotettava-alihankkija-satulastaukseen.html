Key takeaways

  • Before the first load, check the subcontractor's registration details, operator licence and carrier's liability insurance, plus the reports required under the Finnish Contractor's Liability Act where applicable.
  • Every trailer or container swap is a point where liability passes over, and it should be documented with a timestamp, unit numbers, the seal number and photos.
  • Agree the division of liability in writing before signing, even when the subcontractor was found on a freight exchange.
  • Score a new subcontractor on seven questions on a scale of 0–2: under 8 points means you look for someone else.
  • Reassess the subcontractor at 3, 6 and 12 months using the same criteria as at the start.

For trailer and container swaps, you find a reliable subcontractor with three checks: that the business and its licences are legitimate, proven experience of exactly this kind of trailer and container work, and a written division of liability before the first load. This guide gives you a step-by-step assessment process, the liability questions and a printable form, which also work for agreements made via a freight exchange.

How do you find a reliable subcontractor for swaps without unnecessary risk?

The subcontractor runs part of the transport, but towards your customer you are usually the one who is responsible for it: if the subcontractor operates without a valid operator licence or insurance, you may end up bearing the delay, the damage or the consequences yourself. The EU regulation on the occupation of road transport operator requires operators to have, among other things, good repute, appropriate financial standing and professional competence (Regulation (EC) No 1071/2009, EUR-Lex). This guide moves from checking legitimacy to assessing operational capability, liability questions and a ready-made form.

A small haulage firm has no separate vetting team, so the process has to be quick and repeatable. Every new subcontractor goes through the same sequence: paperwork, equipment, references and contract. Once the process is established, assessing one subcontractor takes about half an hour. That is a small effort compared with being left without compensation when a €30,000 load is damaged during a swap.

What do trailer and container swaps mean in terms of liability?

In a swap, a semi-trailer or container changes tractor unit or driver partway through the transport chain. Every swap is a point where liability passes over: the condition of the load, the trailer and the seals is checked, and that check decides who is liable for any damage. If a swap is done without a documented inspection, the evidence is weakest for whoever has the poorest paperwork.

Liability points in a trailer swap

The chain is usually collection, swap, onward transport and handover. For each point there should be a timestamped confirmation showing the registration numbers, the seal number, the number of packages and any discrepancies. Without them, you cannot show that the damage occurred during the subcontractor's leg and not yours. In the RAHTIS driver app, the driver photographs every side of the trailer, the seal and any new damage, and at delivery the photo taken at collection is shown alongside for comparison (RAHTIS and trailer swaps).

The choice of swap location affects the risk: a lit terminal area with CCTV is safer than a dark lay-by. If the subcontractor suggests a swap location without surveillance or lighting, ask why. Agree the swap location in advance and make sure both drivers have access to it.

Special issues in container haulage

With a container, the seal and the container's condition are key evidence: an intact seal, recorded on the consignment note at the time of handover, supports the case that the contents were not tampered with during transport, but on its own it does not decide liability. Ask the subcontractor in advance how they record the seal number and the container inspection. Note that Incoterms, such as FCA or EXW, divide costs and risk between seller and buyer. Liability between you and your subcontractor is decided by the contract of carriage and the applicable law: the CMR Convention for international carriage, and the Finnish Road Transport Contracts Act for domestic carriage.

In container haulage, handling at the port and terminal can cause delays that do not show up in the driver's paperwork. Ask the subcontractor who pays for waiting at the terminal and what happens if a container waits 12 hours for a crane. If the subcontractor cannot answer, they have probably not hauled containers before.

How do you know the subcontractor exists and operates legally?

Before you pass a load on, check a few things. They take a quarter of an hour but prevent the typical pitfalls: the business does not exist, the operator licence is missing or the insurance does not cover the carrier's liability.

Mandatory documents

Ask for copies of the following and file them so they can be found later:

  • Trade register extract: for a Finnish company, the details are available free of charge from YTJ (the Finnish Business Information System); for a foreign company, from the company register in its home country. Check that the company is not bankrupt and that its line of business is transport.
  • VAT number: the VAT identification number of a company from another EU country is checked in the EU's VIES service.
  • Operator licence: in Finland, professional goods transport with a vehicle over 3.5 tonnes requires a goods transport licence issued by Traficom, and international transport requires an EU Community licence. Since 21 May 2022, international transport with vehicles of 2.5–3.5 tonnes has also required a licence. Ask for a copy; do not settle for verbal assurances.
  • Carrier's liability insurance (CMR insurance): general business liability insurance alone does not cover the carrier's liability for the goods carried. Check the maximum cover and validity of the policy.
  • Reports under the Contractor's Liability Act: if the value of the subcontract is at least €9,000 excluding VAT and the work relates to your normal operations, such as regular transports, the Finnish Act on the Contractor's Obligations and Liability when Work is Contracted Out requires you to obtain, before signing, among other things a trade register extract, proof of registration in the prepayment register, the employer register and the VAT register, a tax debt certificate or a statement on tax debt, a pension insurance certificate, and information on the applicable collective agreement and occupational health care (Contractor's Liability Act 1233/2006, Finlex).

If the subcontractor drags their feet over the documents, that is an answer too. A serious subcontractor usually sends the paperwork the same day. If it takes three days to get a copy of the operator licence, consider how quickly they will respond when the load is on the road and something goes wrong.

Credit information

In Finland, payment default records and company financial information are available from credit reference services such as Asiakastieto or Dun & Bradstreet. For a foreign company, equivalent information is available from credit reference services in its own country. Also check whether the company was set up only a few months ago: a new company is not automatically bad, but it warrants a smaller first load and closer monitoring.

A single payment default record is not automatic grounds for rejection, but it changes the terms. Do not make advance payments to a company with outstanding payment defaults. One record may be due to a temporary cash-flow problem, but several records point to a systematic problem.

How do you assess a subcontractor's operational capability in practice?

Operational capability shows in three things: the condition of the equipment, the drivers' language skills and how quickly the subcontractor responds during the day. In trailer swaps, the schedule is everything, so ask directly how quickly you will get a status update on the load. If the answer is "I'll call when I can", keep looking.

Equipment questions

Ask for the registration numbers and to see the latest periodic roadworthiness inspection (katsastus) and a summary of the service history. Check that the tractor unit has a fifth wheel suitable for trailer swaps and the necessary connections. For a one-lorry operator, a single breakdown can stop the whole chain, so ask what happens if the vehicle is stuck in the workshop midweek.

Also ask the age of the equipment. A trailer over 15 years old is not automatically bad, but the brakes and air suspension need closer inspection. If the subcontractor has only one vehicle, make sure they have a contingency plan: their own spare equipment or an agreement with another operator.

Reference questions

Ask for the contact details of two previous clients and call them yourself. Ask three things: did the vehicle leave on schedule, did the proof of delivery come back in order, and was more invoiced than agreed? Record the answers in the same place as the subcontractor's other details.

A reference call is not a formality. Ask concrete questions: was the load on time, what was done when something went wrong, and would the referee use the subcontractor again? If the answer is a hesitant "I suppose so", treat it as a no. If the referee describes precisely how the subcontractor dealt with a delay or damage, that tells you it is a professional operator.

What questions on the division of liability must you ask before the contract?

Liability questions

Ask these before you put a single run in the calendar. Who pays if the load is damaged in a trailer swapped by the subcontractor, or the trailer comes back damaged? Who bears the risk if container handling at the port or terminal causes a delay or damage? How are delays dealt with: who informs the end customer, who pays the extra costs and on what timescale?

Who handles the consignment note and the proof of delivery, and can your customer see them directly? Who is responsible for ADR requirements if the load is dangerous goods? And who bears the risk of an empty return if the subcontractor is left without a backload?

Go through the list in writing, because a verbal "yes, we'll sort it" is no help once the damage has happened. Freight exchanges such as TIMOCOM and Trans.eu describe their own terms of use and member checks on their websites, but they do not replace the contract between you and your subcontractor. In RAHTIS, the job details, photos, consignment note and proof of delivery build up on the same transport, but the division of liability is still agreed in the contract between you.

Contract terms

Write down the terms: liability for damage, the consequences of delay (or the absence of any), payment terms and cancellation terms. A verbal agreement is quick to make, but a written one settles a dispute. Also define what happens if the subcontractor cancels midweek: do they cover the extra cost of a replacement transport, or does each side bear its own losses?

Form template

Scoring

Give each point 0 (no answer), 1 (incomplete) or 2 (in order). A total of 12–14 points means you can send the load. 8–11 points means the gaps are put right in writing first. Under 8 points means you look for another subcontractor.

Do the scoring even when the subcontractor was found on a freight exchange: Trans.eu, TIMOCOM and Teleroute show basic details, but they do not answer these questions for you. When you have to choose between two subcontractors, the scores make the choice justifiable. Without scoring, the choice rests on gut feeling, and gut feeling is a poor justification when the damage exceeds the insurance.

How do subcontracting arrangements made via a freight exchange differ from your own customers?

Platform checks

TIMOCOM, Teleroute and Trans.eu each check their members' company details in their own way, but no platform does the assessment for you. A platform check is a baseline, not a guarantee. Check the company's registration and credit details yourself before the load: in Finland, registration details are available free of charge from YTJ, and payment default information from credit reference services.

The platforms' own ratings and reviews are useful, but the criteria behind them are not always public, and they do not tell you whether the subcontractor's insurance is valid right now. Use the platform's rating as a filter, not as the decision.

What the platform does not cover

Subcontracting via a freight exchange differs from an established working relationship: the division of liability, liability for damage and the special requirements of swaps have to be agreed by you, because the exchange's general terms know nothing about the risk points of a trailer swap. Use the same form as for other subcontractors, and agree the consignment note and proof of delivery practice before the first run. The platform tells you the company exists; you assess whether it handles swaps properly.

How does RAHTIS help with subcontractor management, and where does it fall short?

What RAHTIS takes care of

On the RAHTIS load board, you can see each haulier's vehicle details, ratings, punctuality history and a Verified badge. The badge means that RAHTIS staff have checked the documents the company has submitted under the Contractor's Liability Act, such as the trade register extract, tax and pension insurance certificates and insurance, and recorded the date until which the check is valid. During the transport, photos, seals, the consignment note and the proof of delivery build up on the same job, so documenting swaps does not rely on memory.

What you have to do yourself

The Verified badge tells you the documents are in order, but not whether the subcontractor is right for your particular swaps and containers. Equipment, references, the division of liability and the assessment form remain your job, as covered above. The system shows what has been agreed and what has happened, but you build the trust yourself.

How do you build a long-term subcontractor relationship?

After the first load, the subcontractor has passed the check, but reliability is built through repeated success. That is why the first year is decisive. Reassess the subcontractor at 3, 6 and 12 months using the same criteria as at the start: paperwork, equipment, division of liability and the number of delays.

Keep the assessment form a living document. When the subcontractor runs a load, update the scores. When they are late or the proof of delivery is slow to arrive, record it. After three months you will see the trend: is performance improving or deteriorating? If the trend is bad, do not wait for damage; reduce their loads and move them to a subcontractor who performs better.

A long-term relationship does not mean automatic renewal. It means both sides know what to expect, and the contract is updated when circumstances change. If the insurance is renewed on different terms, ask for a new certificate. If the contract runs for more than 12 months, the Contractor's Liability Act requires the subcontractor to provide tax and pension insurance certificates again every 12 months. If the subcontractor acquires new equipment, check it before the first run.

FAQ

How much should the subcontractor's insurance cover?

Carrier's liability insurance must cover at least the statutory liability: in international carriage, 8.33 special drawing rights (SDR) per kilogram of gross weight of the goods under the CMR Convention, and in domestic carriage €20 per kilogram under the Finnish Road Transport Contracts Act. Ask for the insurance certificate and check its validity. If the value of the load exceeds the liability limit, the consignor can declare a higher value for the goods in the consignment note against an agreed surcharge, or take out separate cargo insurance for the goods. Agree this in writing before the first run.

Can I use a subcontractor found on a freight exchange without doing my own background check?

It is not a good idea. TIMOCOM, Trans.eu and Teleroute check their members' basic details in different ways, but that is a baseline, not a guarantee. Check the registration details, operator licence and insurance certificate yourself, plus the reports under the Contractor's Liability Act where required, and fill in the same assessment form as for other subcontractors.

What do I do if the subcontractor damages a trailer during a swap?

If you have a timestamped confirmation of the swap with photos, unit numbers and the seal number, you can show that the damage occurred during the subcontractor's leg. Without a documented inspection, it is one word against another. That is why the division of liability is agreed in writing in advance, not after the damage.

Do I need a written contract for one-off subcontracting?

Yes. Being a one-off does not remove the liability points: liability for damage, handling of delays, payment terms and cancellation terms should be recorded even for a single run. An email in which the terms have been accepted is often enough. Also agree the consignment note and proof of delivery practice before the run, so the evidence exists if something goes wrong.

How do I check the background of a foreign subcontractor?

Start with the trade register in the company's home country, the equivalent of Finland's YTJ. Check the VAT number in the EU's VIES service if it is an EU company. Ask for a copy of the Community licence for international transport, and look up payment default information from a credit reference service in that country. A new company is not automatically bad, but it warrants a smaller first load and closer monitoring.
