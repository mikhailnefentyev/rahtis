A small haulage firm introduces an electronic driving log by choosing a phone-based logging app, making sure the data meets the statutory requirements, training drivers to log their own journeys and linking the data directly to invoicing and payroll. This guide shows step by step how a firm with 1–12 lorries makes the switch without an IT budget and without finally ditching paper until the electronic version has been proven to work.

Why should a small haulage firm digitise its driving log?

When you are the firm's transport planner, bookkeeper and safety manager all at once, every paper driving log is work you will have to sort out yourself later. An electronic driving log moves the recording to the driver at the moment the journey happens, and the data is ready at the same time for invoicing, payroll and working time monitoring.

Where the paper log breaks down in practice

On paper the handwriting is illegible, hours are missing or the sheet stays in the cab for a week. The requirements for drivers' logs and working time records are laid down in law, and the Finnish Ministry of Economic Affairs and Employment's guidance on working hours requires that the data can be verified afterwards. When the paper is poor, you end up correcting it in the evenings.

Time saved by linking dispatch and invoicing

When journeys, consignment note data and paid hours live in the same system, you do not copy anything twice. RAHTIS (rahtis.eu/fi) combines job tracking, driver shifts and payment tracking for invoices in one place, so the invoice can be made out from the recorded journeys instead of a paper log. The driver report also serves payroll without a separate home-made Excel set-up.

What does the law require of a driving log, and is an electronic version accepted?

Working time legislation and the driver's log

Driving and rest times are recorded by the vehicle's tachograph and on the driver card, but the employer must also keep working time records, which also cover work other than driving, such as loading and waiting. The driving log supplements this: it documents the route, load and mode of transport of each individual job. The law does not specify whether records are kept on paper or electronically, as long as the data is complete, reliable and can be checked afterwards. An electronic driving log is therefore acceptable if it produces the same information as the paper version.

Evidential value and archiving of electronic data

Evidential value is decisive in disputes: a customer questions the delivery time or a shipper questions the content of the consignment note. An electronic log contains a time stamp, the driver's identifier and an unalterable history, which is clearer evidence than paper. Retention obligations are worth checking with your own accountant, but in practice the data has to be kept on file for several years. In RAHTIS the journey data, consignment note and POD are linked to the same job, so the evidence is in one place rather than in separate folders (https://www.rahtis.eu/et). Remember that it must also be possible to show the driver's signature and the vehicle identifier afterwards.

What does an electronic driving log cost a small firm?

A realistic budget for a firm with 1–10 vehicles is from tens to hundreds of euros a month. The cost is made up of the software licence, the drivers' phones and your own time: installation, training and changing habits often cost more than the licences.

Hidden costs: training, devices, integrations

Three items are often left out of the budget. Training drivers takes working days, and every new person logging needs to be inducted again. Devices: if logging is done on the driver's own phone, the cost is zero; company tablets cost €200–400 each. Integrations with invoicing and payroll are free when built in, but expensive when custom-made.

Remember the archiving obligation too: under Article 10 of Regulation (EC) No 561/2006 on driving and rest times, tachograph and driver card data must be downloaded regularly (the driver card at least every 28 days and the vehicle unit every 90 days, Commission Regulation (EU) No 581/2010) and kept for at least 12 months, and under Directive 2002/15/EC working time records must be kept for at least two years. So choose a service you can also get your data out of after the contract ends (EU Regulation 561/2006, EUR-Lex).

How do you choose an electronic driving log without an IT department?

Selection criteria for a small firm

When you are the transport planner, invoicing clerk and safety manager yourself, choose a solution against five criteria: can you use it on a phone without training, do you see entries in real time, does the data go straight to invoicing, does it work without a separate server, and is the pricing per vehicle rather than per user? Test each candidate on one real job, not a demo: log one loose trailer move, one consignment note and one empty run from start to finish. If installation needs a consultant or hours of learning, give it a miss.

How do you get drivers on board with the new way of logging journeys?

Mobile logging that copes with terminal routines

Logging is done on a phone, with gloves on, dirty screens and a poor signal. The solution must work offline, open with a single tap and ask only for the essential fields: origin, destination, load and job status. If logging takes more than a minute, it will not get done and paper will go back in the pocket.

Allow drivers their own way of working too: one logs at every stop, another at the end of the day. What matters most is that the data is created out on the road, not afterwards in the office from memory.

Ground rules for the transition period

Run both in parallel for two weeks: paper and electronic logging at the same time. This gives you comparison data and drivers learn without pressure. Agree on one rule: notes may be written on paper, but the official record is electronic, and missing entries are sorted out the same day, not a week later.

Remember the archiving obligation: in Finland electronic documents must be kept for the period laid down in the Accounting Act, and their evidential value requires the data to be unalterable and traceable (Vero.fi, the Finnish Tax Administration, on keeping electronic records). Once logging is in order, the same data is ready in RAHTIS for invoicing, and you can look for jobs for free vehicles on the RAHTIS offer desk; you can join via the application page.

How does driving log data get to invoicing and payroll?

Journey data to the consignment note and the invoice

When journey logging is electronic, the same entry can work in three places: as the driver's log, as an attachment to the consignment note and as the basis for invoicing. In practice the driver logs the job, the kilometres and waiting times, and the system links them to the order. In RAHTIS the consignment note and journey data are in the same workflow, so the invoice can be raised directly from the logged journey without copying from paper into Excel.

An honest limitation: RAHTIS does not send freight invoices through an e-invoicing operator. You make out the invoice in your own invoicing or financial management software and mark it on the job in RAHTIS, where the payment status and reminders are then handled. That is still quicker than typing it up from paper notes.

Working time and payroll

The same entry also works as a working time report. The driver's journeys, breaks and other work are saved with time stamps, and at the end of the month you send a ready-made report to payroll instead of a stack of paper slips. Digital logging is also more accurate: checks based on the vehicle's speed data reveal missing entries that on paper only come to light at inspection.

If you use separate payroll software, check before choosing which file format you can use to transfer working time data into it.

How do you move from paper to electronic step by step?

Six weeks is enough if the job is split into three two-week blocks: selection and pilot, parallel running and finally retiring paper. The aim is that no job goes unlogged by falling into a gap between the two systems during the transition.

Weeks 1–2: selection and pilot

Choose a solution using the criteria described earlier and put one driver on trial. Only one lorry's journeys are logged electronically; paper is not removed yet. At the same time, check the guidance from the industry association SKAL so the data you record is correct from the start: keeping a driving log. In week 2, go over with the pilot driver what worked and what felt like pointless tapping.

Weeks 3–4: parallel running

All vehicles now log both on paper and electronically. This is the most time-consuming phase, but it exposes the situations where the app does not fit the real work: waiting for loading, no signal in the yard, the change of day falling in the middle of a job. Compare the paper and electronic versions daily and fix logging habits immediately rather than collecting corrections on a list.

Weeks 5–6: retiring paper for good

The paper version stays in the vehicle as a backup, but it is no longer filled in. Drivers log only electronically, and you check the entries at the end of the day before invoicing. Once two full weeks have gone by with no missing entries and invoices have been produced from the driving log data without any separate reconciling, paper can be dropped completely. Keep one completed paper form as a reference point in case the tax authority or an inspector asks questions later.

Summary: where to start today?

Pick one vehicle and one driver, and log next week electronically alongside paper. Check that the data flows straight from the consignment note to invoicing without retyping. If everything goes through, extend it to a second vehicle and drop paper altogether within a month.

FAQ

Can the driving log still be kept on paper if drivers refuse to use a mobile app?

Yes, because the law does not prescribe the form of the records, as long as the data is complete and can be checked afterwards. Paper, however, requires constant correcting and chasing: illegible handwriting, missing hours and late forms end up being fixed by you. Two weeks of parallel running lowers the threshold, and most drivers get used to logging on a phone when it takes less than a minute and works offline.

Does an electronic driving log work without a signal at a terminal or abroad?

Yes, if you choose an app that logs offline and uploads the data later. The signal at terminals is often weak, so the log must open with a single tap and save locally on the phone. Test this during the pilot on one real job, not a demo: log one loose trailer move and one empty run from start to finish. If logging gets stuck waiting for a connection, paper will soon be back in the cab.

Does a small firm need separate driving log software, or is a TMS with logging built in enough?

A separate driving log program rarely makes sense for a firm with 1–12 lorries. It is enough for logging to be part of the same workflow as the consignment note and invoicing, so the same entry serves the driving log, the invoice and payroll without copying. Choose a solution against five criteria: phone use without training, real-time visibility, direct transfer to invoicing, no server, and per-vehicle pricing.

How long must driving log data be kept by law?

The retention period depends on the data. Under Regulation (EC) No 561/2006 on driving and rest times, data downloaded from the tachograph and driver card must be kept for at least 12 months; under Directive 2002/15/EC working time records must be kept for at least two years; and under the Finnish Accounting Act data used as invoicing vouchers must be kept for at least six years from the end of the year in which the financial year ended. Check your own situation with your accountant, and before choosing, make sure you can get all your data out of the service even after the contract ends, so that the archiving obligation does not depend on the app.

How are incorrect entries made by drivers corrected afterwards?

Agree a rule that missing or wrongly logged journeys are sorted out the same day, not a week later. In a good system a correction leaves a time-stamped trace in the unalterable history, which preserves the evidential value of the records in a dispute. Check the entries yourself at the end of the day before invoicing, so errors do not make it onto the invoice and the driver gets feedback straight away.
