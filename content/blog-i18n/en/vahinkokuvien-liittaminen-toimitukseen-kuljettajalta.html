The driver attaches damage photos to a delivery in the RAHTIS driver app by opening the job, pressing Arrived at the delivery point, photographing the damaged area during the inspection and marking it as new damage before the stop is marked as done. This guide covers the steps, photo quality and what to do when the signal is weak at a port or terminal.

When must damage photos be attached to the delivery?

Damage photos are taken at the delivery point before or during unloading, before the consignee signs and at the latest before the stop is marked as done. When the photo is saved to the same job straight away, it shows what condition the goods and equipment were in at the moment of delivery, and nobody has to guess whether the damage happened only after unloading in the consignee's warehouse.

Timing also matters because of the time limits. Under section 40 of the Finnish Road Transport Contracts Act (345/1979), which for international carriage corresponds to Article 30 of the CMR Convention, the consignee must make reservations about apparent damage no later than at the time of delivery, and about other damage in writing within seven days, excluding Sundays and public holidays. In international carriage, the absence of reservations means that the goods are deemed to have been delivered in the condition described in the consignment note, unless proven otherwise. In domestic carriage, failing to make reservations generally means losing the right to bring a claim (Road Transport Contracts Act, Finlex). A photo timestamped at the moment of delivery is stronger evidence than one sent a couple of days later.

A practical rule of thumb for drivers: take the photos during unloading, record the damage in the app and on the consignment note straight away, and only then take the consignee's signature. That way the signed proof of delivery and the damage photos are on the same job.

How does the driver attach damage photos to the delivery, step by step?

The driver takes the photos on their phone in the RAHTIS driver app, with no separate messaging apps or email. The app works in the driver's own language (including Finnish, Swedish, English, Estonian, Latvian, Lithuanian, Polish and Russian), so the same instructions work for the whole team. The instructions apply both to jobs for your own customers and to transports taken from the RAHTIS load board.

Step 1: Open the job and confirm arrival

The driver opens the task and presses Arrived at the delivery point. The arrival time and location are saved on the job with the same tap. In the Note on the equipment or load field, the driver briefly records what has been damaged and where, so there is no trying to remember the details in the evening.

Step 2: Take photos during the inspection and mark new damage

Under Inspection and photos, the driver photographs every side of the trailer or container, the seal and the load, taking extra photos if necessary. If the driver sees new damage, they mark it with the New damage option before taking the photo. The photo of the same side taken at collection is shown alongside, so an old dent is not accidentally recorded as new. Photos are taken with the app's Take photo button, so they are saved directly to the right job and stop. When photos are sent later by email or a messaging app, they easily end up on the wrong job or stay on the driver's phone.

Step 3: Proof of delivery and a final check before the stop is marked as done

Before pressing Mark as done, the driver can see the photos taken at that stop. If a photo is missing or unclear, it is retaken on the spot. As proof of delivery, the consignee signs on the screen along with their name, or the driver scans the signed consignment note. Once the stop is done, the photos, the note and the confirmation are visible to the haulier in the job details without any separate messaging.

What quality is required of damage photos?

A damage photo is not evidence if the damage cannot be seen in it. The insurer and the shipper compare the photos with the consignment note and other documents, and an unclear photo gives them grounds to dispute the claim.

Three photos: overall view, close-up, identifier

Take at least three photos. The first shows the whole damaged side, so the extent of the damage can be assessed. The second is a close-up of the damage, close enough to make out the crack, dent or tear. The third shows an identifier: the registration number, container number, trailer number or seal number, which ties the photo to this particular transport.

The most common quality problems: blur, glare, close-up only

Blur is the most common reason for an unusable photo. In a dim building, steady the phone or take the photo twice. Glare is another pitfall, especially on a container's sheet-metal surface: move to one side so that the sun or a lamp does not hide the damage. A close-up on its own, without an overall shot, leaves the location of the damage unclear.

The driver does not need to worry about image size: before sending, the app scales the photo down to about 1,600 pixels on the longer side. The damage and the seal number remain legible, but the file is many times lighter than the phone's original photo and goes through even on a slow connection.

Why does attaching a photo fail, and how do you fix it?

When a photo is not saved, the cause is usually one of these: camera access has been blocked, the phone's storage is full or the connection has dropped. They can usually be fixed on the phone in a few minutes.

Camera access

The RAHTIS driver app is a web app installed on the phone's home screen via the browser, not from an app store. If the driver has at some point denied camera access, allow it again in the browser's site settings: on Android, in the Chrome menu under Settings → Site settings → Camera; on iPhone, under Settings → Safari → Camera. Full storage also prevents a new photo from being taken, so delete old videos or downloads.

Updating the app

The app updates itself when it is opened with a network connection, so no separate update from an app store is needed. If the screen freezes, close the app and open it again. Do not log out if there are still unsent photos in the queue.

What should you do if the signal is weak or photos will not upload?

Take the photos first; the queue takes care of sending

A weak signal does not stop you taking photos. Take all the photos you need in the app as normal. The app stores the entries and photos in a queue on the phone and sends them in the order they were made once the connection returns. The time of the tap goes with them, so a late upload does not change the recorded time of delivery. The top of the screen shows that there is no connection, and queued entries are marked.

If the signal is gone for a long time, you can drive on and let the queue clear once the lorry is back in coverage or on Wi-Fi. Tell drivers in advance that queued entries do not need to be redone and that there is no need to log in to the app again.

How to make sure the photos have arrived

Once the queue is empty and the connection notice has gone, the photos are on the server. The haulier can see them in the job details. If you are unsure, check the job in the office view before the driver deletes anything from the phone.

The handling of photos and other delivery documents is described in the RAHTIS terms of use and privacy notice.

Who sees the attached damage photos, and what happens to them?

Once the driver has photographed the damage, the haulier's dispatcher can see the photos in the job details as soon as they have been sent. The shipper who ordered the job sees the arrivals, confirmations, photos and locations in the transport tracking, so there is no need to ask for photos separately via a messaging app. The photos are saved on the job with the time and place they were taken, so it is clear afterwards at what stage the damage was recorded.

Photos as part of the proof of delivery

The damage photos are on the same job as the proof of delivery (POD) and the scanned consignment note. If a claim is made for the damage, the photos show what condition the goods were in at the moment of delivery. What is decisive, however, are the notes and reservations made on the consignment note or another document, so a photo supports the note but does not replace it.

What the dispatcher should do straight away

Open the job and check that the photos are sharp and the damaged area is visible. If photos are missing or unclear, contact the driver immediately, before the lorry has left the site. Notify the shipper of the damage without delay, so the claims procedure can start within the time limits.

How do damage photos differ from ordinary delivery photos?

An ordinary inspection photo and the proof of delivery document that the delivery took place: goods delivered, consignee signed, unit number visible. A damage photo documents a deviation: a dent, a break, a damp mark or a missing package. Both are on the same job, but they record different things.

An ordinary confirmation is enough when the delivery goes as it should. A damage photo must always be accompanied by a written note on the consignment note or a separate damage report, because a photo on its own does not show who was responsible when the damage occurred.

When the same delivery needs both

If damage to the goods is visible on unloading, the driver photographs it before the consignee signs for the delivery and records their reservations. The same job then holds the damage photos, the proof of delivery and the consignment note on which the damage has been noted. The delivery can be invoiced even while the damage is in dispute, and each party has its own evidence: you have the photos, the consignee has the reservation.

A practical rule for drivers: inspection photos are always taken; a damage entry is made only when something is out of the ordinary. Everything is saved to the same job, so later on nobody has to guess which photo belonged to which load.

FAQ

Can I attach damage photos afterwards, once the delivery has already been marked as done?

Photos should always be taken before the stop is marked as done. A photo sent later has weaker evidential value, because it does not show that the damage already existed at the moment of delivery. The statutory time limits also apply to the consignee's reservations: for apparent damage, they must be made no later than at delivery.

How many damage photos should be attached to a delivery?

An overall shot, a close-up of the damage and a photo of the identifier are usually enough. Extra photos can be taken if there are several areas of damage. Photograph the damage, not the whole load.

Does attaching photos work without a connection at the port?

Yes. Take the photos in the app as normal. They stay in a queue on the phone and are sent automatically when the connection returns, and the recorded time is the moment of the tap. Do not redo the entries, even if they appear in the queue.

Can I use the phone's own camera instead of the app?

It is not a good idea. Photos taken with the phone's camera stay in the gallery and have to be sent separately. They then easily end up on the wrong job or never get sent at all. A photo taken with the app is saved directly to the right stop with time and location.

What do I do if the driver sent the wrong photo?

Contact the driver immediately, before the lorry has left, and ask them to retake the correct photo in the app. Record this in the job's messages, so the shipper and the haulier see the same information.
