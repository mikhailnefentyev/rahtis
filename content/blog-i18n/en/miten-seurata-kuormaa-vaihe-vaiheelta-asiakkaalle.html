Key takeaways

  • All the customer needs to know is what stage the transport is at: confirmed, loaded, in transit, arrived, and delivered and signed for.
  • In RAHTIS, the customer opens the tracking page from a link without needing their own login, and sees the timestamped stages and the estimated time of arrival.
  • After delivery, the same page shows the consignment note, the name of the person who signed for the goods and photos from the delivery point, so nobody has to dig receipts out of their email.
  • The tracking page does not show the vehicle's position on a map, the driver's phone number or the freight rate; GPS data are used only to estimate the time of arrival.
  • You see delays yourself as alerts, and the customer sees the updated arrival estimate via the link; a shipper with its own system can receive events through the API.

The customer wants to know two things: what stage the transport is at and when it will arrive. When the driver confirms the stages in the app, the RAHTIS tracking link shows them to the customer with timestamps, so the customer does not need to call to ask where the load is. This guide goes through the stages and how to share tracking with your customer.

How does the customer order a transport and get access to tracking?

A job is created in RAHTIS when you enter your own customer's order, or forward the customer's order message as an email, PDF or image, from which the system creates a draft. When you enter the customer's email address, the customer receives a message saying who is handling the transport, with a link to the tracking page. From then on, the customer can see from the link what stage the transport is at without calling you. The features are summarised on the RAHTIS website.

The order confirmation is the first stage of tracking

The confirmation fixes what is being carried, when it is collected and where it is delivered. It is always worth confirming in writing, and in RAHTIS every job has its own number that stays with it throughout the transport. The tracking page shows when the transport was confirmed.

What the customer needs to provide when ordering

At a minimum: the collection and delivery addresses, the requested time, details of the goods and a contact person at each end. If the transport involves a trailer swap or a container, it should be recorded in the order straight away so that the stages are set up correctly. It is a good idea to agree the consignment note details at the same time.

What stages does a transport go through?

From the customer's point of view, a transport has five stages: confirmed, loaded, in transit, arrived and delivered. Each stage tells the customer one thing: where things stand and when to expect the next change.

The stages in a table

StageWhat it meansWhat the customer sees on the tracking page
ConfirmedThe haulier has confirmed the job and assigned a vehicleThe haulier, the route and the planned times
LoadedThe driver has confirmed the loading point as completedArrival and departure times at the loading point
In transitThe vehicle is on its way to the next stopThe estimated time of arrival and what it is based on
ArrivedThe driver has arrived at the delivery pointThe arrival time
DeliveredUnloading has been signed forThe consignment note, the name of the person who signed and photos from the delivery point

When the stage changes and who changes it

The driver confirms the stages in the RAHTIS driver app: arrival and loading at the loading point, and arrival and unloading at the delivery point, where the driver takes the consignee's signature and photos. The tracking page updates according to these confirmations, without you having to pass on any information.

The tracking link can be forwarded, and anyone who receives it sees only this one transport. The tracking page is shown in the language selected in the link, and RAHTIS works in ten languages, including Swedish, Estonian, Latvian and Lithuanian.

Can the customer see the load's position in real time?

Not on a map. The RAHTIS tracking page shows an arrival estimate, not the vehicle's position. If a GPS fleet tracking system is connected to the vehicle (for example Wialon, Mapon, Webfleet or ABAX via an API key), the arrival estimate is calculated from the vehicle's latest position and the traffic situation. Otherwise the estimate is based on the route plan or the time given by the haulier, and the page states what the estimate is based on.

What the tracking page shows and what it does not

The page shows the haulier, the route, the stages with timestamps, the estimated time of arrival and, on Finnish roads, traffic announcements and road weather along the route. The vehicle's coordinates, the driver's phone number and the freight rate are not on the page. The position of a vehicle driven by an identifiable driver is personal data, so sharing it is restricted by the EU General Data Protection Regulation.

The driver's role

The driver does not need a separate device: they confirm the stages in the driver app, which also works offline and sends the confirmations once the connection returns. The app does not send a continuous position; the location data for the arrival estimate come from the vehicle's own tracking system, if one is connected.

How does information about delivery and delays get through?

Delivery and proof of delivery on the same page

Once unloading has been signed for, the consignment note, the name of the person who signed and photos from the delivery point appear on the tracking page. The driver takes them with the app, so you do not have to send PDFs as email attachments. The customer can see straight away what was delivered and when. The same job carries on into invoicing: when you create an invoice for your own customer, the sign-off and the consignment note are already on the job.

This does away with the situation where the customer calls asking for the signed delivery note, you search through a pile of paper and send a photo by WhatsApp an hour later.

Delays flagged before the customer has time to call

When a vehicle is running behind the planned time, you see it in RAHTIS as a delay alert, and the customer sees the updated arrival estimate on the tracking page. A shipper with its own system can also receive a webhook notification from RAHTIS, for example when the arrival estimate changes or the vehicle is more than 30 minutes late at a stop. When a delay is significant, it is still worth calling or writing to the customer yourself: a personal message about the new schedule is better service than just a changed time.

How do I track a transport involving a trailer swap or a container?

A multi-stage transport is not one journey but a chain: collection, a swap or terminal, and final delivery. When each point is on the job as its own waypoint, you can see at once where the chain has got to.

Tracking a trailer swap

When a semi-trailer changes tractor unit, the swap point is entered on the route as a stop in its own right. The customer sees in the tracking that the trailer is at the swap point and does not assume it has gone missing. When the swap point is confirmed, the tracking page updates without a separate phone call. More information on trailer swaps is on the page RAHTIS: trailer swap.

Container haulage from the port to the destination

In container haulage, the same logic applies from the pick-up point onwards: collecting the container from the port, the journey and delivery are separate stages, each shown with its own timestamp. You can share the tracking link with your customer, who can see for themselves whether the container is still at the port or on its way.

How do I share tracking with my own customer?

Tracking information does not stay only on your screen. Enter the customer's email address on the job and they will receive the tracking link, or send the link yourself. The customer sees the stages for themselves and does not call to ask where the load is.

Tracking without an email chain

Traditionally, the chain works like this: the shipper calls you, you call the driver and the information travels back by phone. When the driver confirms the stages in the app, the information appears on the tracking page without anyone having to pass it on by hand.

When your customer's customer also wants to track

If you haul for a freight forwarder or a larger haulier, their customer may also want to see the status of the transport. The shipper can forward the tracking link. Whoever opens the link sees only this transport, not the rest of your fleet or your other jobs. Remember that the link works for anyone it is forwarded to, so share it only with those who are meant to see the transport details.

What do I do if tracking does not update?

The most common causes and quick fixes

Most gaps are caused by one of three things: the driver has not yet confirmed the stage, the driver's phone has no network connection, or the vehicle's tracking device has not sent its position. First check the job's stages: if the load has been confirmed as loaded but the arrival estimate is not updating, the cause is usually the tracking device or its connection, and the estimate is then based on the route plan. The driver app stores confirmations even without a network and sends them when the connection returns, so a short dead zone does not interrupt tracking.

When to contact the driver

Call the driver if a stage has not updated as expected or the customer is already asking about the load. Ask for the sign-off and photos as soon as they arrive, so invoicing is not held up.

What does tracking cost, and how does it fit with the rest of transport management?

Per-vehicle pricing

RAHTIS is priced per vehicle, not per user: the first vehicle is free, and each additional vehicle that has run jobs during the month costs €14.90 a month (excl. VAT). Drivers use the app at no extra charge, and the tracking link is included in the price. An idle vehicle costs nothing, and no percentage is charged on jobs. For fleets of 100 vehicles or more, the price is agreed by quotation.

Tracking as part of the whole workflow

Tracking is not a separate add-on but part of the same job: entry, loading, transit, any swap, delivery and sign-off. Once the sign-off has been saved, you can invoice your own customer for the job and track payment status in the same place. The information flows from order to payment without tracking data having to be copied separately for the customer or the accountant.

FAQ

Can my customer see the tracking without their own login?

Yes. The customer opens the tracking page from the link without logging in. The page shows the stages with timestamps, the estimated time of arrival and, after delivery, the proof of delivery. The link can be forwarded, and whoever opens it sees only this transport.

Is the vehicle shown on a map all the time?

No. The tracking page has no map of the vehicle's position. If a GPS tracking system is connected to the vehicle, its latest position is used to calculate the arrival estimate, and the page shows when the estimate was last updated. Without a tracking device, the estimate is based on the route plan or the time given by the haulier.

Does tracking also work for transports in Sweden or the Baltics?

Yes. The stages and the tracking link work for international transports too, because they are based on the driver's confirmations. Traffic and road weather information is shown only for Finnish roads. RAHTIS works in ten languages, so you can share the tracking page with the customer in their own language.

Does the customer get the proof of delivery with the invoice or separately?

The customer can see the consignment note, the name of the person who signed and the delivery point photos on the tracking page immediately after delivery. When you create the invoice, the sign-off and the consignment note are already on the same job, so there is no need to dig out PDFs or WhatsApp photos.

Can I track several loads in the same view?

Yes. You see all jobs in progress and their stages in the same view, so you know at once what is in transit, what is at a swap point and what is awaiting sign-off. Delay and standstill alerts tell you which job to deal with first.
