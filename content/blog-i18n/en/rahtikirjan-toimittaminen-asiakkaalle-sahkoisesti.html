Yes, you can: a consignment note can be sent to the customer electronically, either as a PDF copy or as an electronic consignment note. For domestic carriage, electronic consignment notes are permitted under the Finnish Road Transport Contracts Act, and for international carriage the e-CMR is based on the Additional Protocol to the CMR Convention, administered by the United Nations Economic Commission for Europe (UNECE). This guide takes you through delivery step by step, signing and retention.

Can a consignment note be sent to the customer electronically?

Yes. For domestic carriage in Finland, issuing a consignment note is not even mandatory, and under a provision added to the Road Transport Contracts Act (345/1979) in 2019, a consignment note may also be issued electronically. In international road haulage, an electronic consignment note, or e-CMR, is possible when both the country of departure and the country of destination have acceded to the Additional Protocol to the CMR Convention.

The Additional Protocol to the CMR Convention permits electronic consignment notes

The Additional Protocol on the electronic consignment note was concluded in Geneva in 2008 and entered into force internationally in 2011. It entered into force for Finland on 11 April 2019. Countries that have acceded include Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Estonia, Germany and Poland, but not all EU countries, so check the destination country before you give up paper. The text of the Additional Protocol and the list of contracting states are available on the UNECE website.

When a PDF is enough and when you need an e-CMR

A PDF that you scan and email to the customer is in practice a copy of the paper: the signature is an image, and you cannot show that the document has not been altered. That is enough for many regular customers, but it is not an e-CMR. If the customer or consignee requires an e-CMR under the Additional Protocol, you need a dedicated e-CMR service. RAHTIS does not issue e-CMRs under the Additional Protocol: the driver takes the signature and photos in the app and attaches the scanned consignment note to the job, and after delivery the customer can see them via the tracking link.

PDF or e-CMR: what difference does it make when sending to the customer?

In short: a scanned or PDF consignment note is acceptable to many customers, but its evidential value in a dispute or an inspection is weaker than that of an e-CMR signed within a system. A PDF is a picture of a document; an e-CMR is the document itself, with its signatures and timestamps stored in the system.

PDF: quick, but weaker as evidence

If you photograph the paper consignment note and send the customer a PDF, delivery is quick. The problem is that a PDF does not reliably show when and by whom it was signed. If the customer disputes the delivery or payment is delayed, you will be relying on the paper original, so keep it.

e-CMR: signature and timestamp in the system

An e-CMR is an electronic consignment note under the Additional Protocol, signed by the parties to the contract of carriage within the system, with every change remaining visible together with its timestamp. The customer can see the signatures in the system immediately after unloading.

How do you send an electronic consignment note to the customer, step by step?

The process has four steps: issuing the consignment note, the driver's sign-off, delivery to the customer and attaching the proof of delivery (POD) to the invoice. Once the steps are in place, the same routine repeats on every job.

Step 1: issuing the consignment note before collection

Issue the consignment note before the lorry leaves for collection. Enter the consignor, consignee, goods details and agreed terms once; do not copy a paper template by hand. If the job is already in the system, the basic details are ready.

Step 2: the driver's sign-off at collection and delivery

The driver sees the job on their phone and confirms collection and delivery. The consignee signs for receipt on the driver's device, so the proof of delivery is created directly in the system.

Step 3: delivery channel – email, tracking link or e-CMR service

Choose the delivery channel according to what the customer accepts. A PDF sent by email is a copy of the paper: it is easy to read, but it is not an e-CMR. A genuine electronic consignment note is created in an e-CMR service, where the data are stored together with the signatures and every change is logged, so either party can show what the consignment note said and when it was confirmed. Large consignees, such as manufacturers and retail chains, may require an e-CMR; smaller ones are often content with a PDF. Find out from each regular customer what they accept, and do not run two parallel processes for the same job.

Step 4: attaching the proof of delivery to the invoice

Once delivery has been signed for, attach the consignment note and proof of delivery to the invoice. Under the Finnish Accounting Act (1336/1997), vouchers such as a sales invoice and its attachments must be kept for at least six years from the end of the year in which the financial year ended. In RAHTIS, the sign-off, photos and image of the consignment note are on the job, and the invoice created from the job goes to the customer as a PDF attachment.

What is required of an e-CMR system?

An e-CMR must be based on the Additional Protocol to the CMR Convention. Under the Additional Protocol, the parties authenticate the electronic consignment note with a reliable electronic signature linked to it, and the system must ensure that the document retains its integrity and cannot be altered without detection.

Signature and integrity

Each party, i.e. the consignor, the carrier and the consignee, confirms its own stage, and the system records who confirmed it and when. Once the consignment note has been confirmed, its content can no longer be changed without detection: corrections are shown separately. This is exactly what distinguishes an e-CMR from an ordinary PDF that anyone can edit.

Identifying the driver and the other parties

Identification determines whether a signature can be linked to the right person. The driver must log in to the system with their own credentials, not a shared company login. The same applies to the consignor and the consignee. In RAHTIS, each driver gets their own login, and the sign-off is recorded under the driver's name. How long must an electronic consignment note be kept?

Retention periods under the Accounting Act

Under Chapter 2, section 10 of the Finnish Accounting Act (1336/1997), vouchers must be kept for at least six years from the end of the year in which the financial year ended, and the financial statements and books of account for at least ten years from the end of the financial year. When a consignment note or proof of delivery is attached to a sales invoice, it forms part of the vouchers, so six years is the minimum. The format does not affect the retention period: the same applies to paper and electronic records. For claims arising under the contract of carriage, it may be sensible to keep the consignment note even longer.

Electronic archiving: legibility and integrity

Records kept electronically must remain legible, and printable if necessary, throughout the retention period, and the method of storage must be reliable. In practice this means a durable file format, backups and a system from which the document can still be retrieved years later. If your electronic archive is in a service provider's system, make sure you can export the records even after the contract ends.

How does an electronic consignment note speed up invoicing in a small firm?

When the consignment note and sign-off are electronic, the proof of delivery does not stay in the driver's pocket or a paper folder. It is stored on the job at the time of delivery, and the invoice can be sent as soon as the load has been delivered. In a paper-based chain, the same cycle takes days or weeks.

Proof of delivery attached to the job

Each job is a self-contained record: consignment note, proof of delivery and agreed price in one place. When the sign-off comes in, it is linked directly to that job, and the invoice can be raised without a separate folder or digging through emails.

Who pays late, and why the document is missing

Late payment is often caused by a missing proof of delivery, not by unwillingness to pay. When the document is missing, chasing payment turns into an investigation: calls, emails and resending. With an electronic chain, you can see for yourself which jobs are missing a sign-off and sort it out before the due date.

What should you do in the first week of switching to electronic consignment notes?

FAQ

Can I send the consignment note to the customer by WhatsApp or email?

You can, but then you are sending a copy, not an e-CMR. A copy is easy to read, but its origin and integrity are hard to prove after the event. Keep the original and ask each regular customer what they accept.

Is a photo of the consignment note taken by the driver acceptable?

A photo is a copy, just like a PDF sent by email. It is good everyday evidence of delivery, but it is not an e-CMR and does not replace the signed original. A genuine electronic consignment note is created in a system where the parties sign electronically and every change is logged.

Is an electronic signature required, or is the driver's sign-off in the app enough?

It depends on what you need. As everyday proof of delivery, a sign-off made with the driver's own credentials plus the consignee's signature is often enough, because the system records who signed and when. An e-CMR under the Additional Protocol, however, requires a reliable electronic signature that identifies the signatory and is linked to the consignment note in such a way that any changes are detectable.
